Why Refusing to (See, Listen or Speak "Evil") can be positive?

2026-05-24 — —

First Lesson: Learn to Deny Orders. Accept suggestions, Accept Critics, Deny Orders!

Second Lesson: You are Free. You can make a Choice.

Third Lesson: Your Mindset and Your Will to change, is the Key to archive any gold.

The image everybody knows and nobody reads

Three monkeys, one covering his eyes, one covering his ears, one covering his mouth. The image travels around the world as a decoration, printed on small statues and sold in airports, and almost always it is read as a lesson in obedience: do not see evil, do not hear evil, do not speak evil. Be quiet, be useful, and let the world happen to somebody else.

That reading never convinced me. A monkey with his hands over his eyes is not blind, he is choosing. The gesture is active. It says: this, for now, does not enter. And a choice about what enters the mind is the first form of discipline that any person can practice, because attention is the only door we can close without asking permission.

To see everything is to be at the mercy of everything

A mind that cannot close a door is not open, it is unprotected. The endless feed, the notification, the argument of a stranger at three in the morning, the news that repeats itself every hour with a new face: none of it asks whether we have room for it. It simply arrives, and the mind that says yes to everything ends up owned by everything.

So the first monkey is not refusing knowledge. He is refusing noise, which is a different substance. Knowledge has a shape, a source and a reason to exist. Noise has an urgency. Learning to tell them apart takes years and it is the most profitable skill I know.

Orders and suggestions are not the same thing

A suggestion offers a reason and leaves the decision with me. A critic offers a correction and leaves the decision with me. An order removes the decision, and a person who only follows orders never learns to judge, because judgement is the exact part that was taken away.

This is why the first lesson is about refusing without guilt. I am not telling anyone to be rude to a teacher, a parent or a boss. I am saying that the sentence do it because I say so is the sentence that makes a person replaceable. The ability to say no to an order is the same ability that lets someone say no to a bad idea, and a society of people who cannot refuse is a society that can be pointed anywhere by whoever speaks loudest.

Accept the suggestion, consider the critic, refuse the order. In that order, every time. Not because authority is always wrong, but because authority without a reason is not authority yet, it is only volume.

Freedom is a verb with a price

Freedom is not the absence of limits. It is the presence of a choice, which implies that there is more than one path and that the person is the one who walks. Without that, freedom is only a word used in speeches, and the speeches are usually written by people who would prefer that we did not use it.

The second monkey covers his ears because some voices are not offering a path, they are offering a direction. A direction comes with a promised reward: the correct opinion, the correct career, the correct life. A path comes with work. The difference is not in the volume of the voice, it is in whether the voice survives the question why.

The third monkey, and the gold that is not money

The third one covers his mouth, and people read it as silence before injustice. I read it as economy. Words spent on what cannot be changed are words taken from what can. The mouth is not sealed, it is reserved for the sentence that deserves to exist.

The gold of the third lesson is not money. It is whatever a person builds when nobody is deciding for him: a habit, a craft, a text, a family, a piece of software, a garden. The mindset is the key, the will is the hand that turns it, and the door opens on the other side of the noise that the three monkeys decided to ignore.

How to practice it this week

Nothing here works as a theory. It works as a repetition, and it is small enough to start today:

  • Pick one source of noise and close it for seven days. Not forever. Seven days.
  • When someone gives you an order, ask for the reason. If the reason exists, follow it with more will than before.
  • When someone gives you a label, wait a day before accepting it. Labels age badly.
  • Choose one thing to build that nobody asked for, and give it twenty minutes a day.

That is the whole discipline. The monkey does not refuse the world. He refuses to be carried by it, and in that small distance between the world and the mind there is room enough for a life.
