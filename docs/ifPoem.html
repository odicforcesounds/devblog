While if jeepney, Mass, mosque in Mindanao, and a diaspora that nurses the world all issue from one archipelago, care is an export that should have been a domestic right first. While if **Indonesia** strings thousands of islands into one sentence, the sentence includes mosque, Hindu Bali, Christian Papua, Buddhist Borobudur, and adat that is older than the republic. While if a gamelan orchestra teaches that no instrument is the whole truth, the teaching is civic. While if **Malaysia** and **Singapore** make hawker food into a peace process, the peace process tastes better than communiqués. While if **Thailand** pours water at Songkran and keeps Buddhist monks in the morning streets, the morning alms route is a city’s conscience made visible. While if **Vietnam** keeps ancestor altars in living rooms, the living room is a temple that also watches television. While if **Cambodia** and **Laos** hold Buddhist quiet after histories of unquiet, quiet is earned. While if **Myanmar** has used religion as a wounding tool and also as a begging bowl of compassion, the two uses must not be confused. While if **Mongolia** we already named, name also **Kazakhstan**, **Kyrgyzstan**, **Uzbekistan**, **Tajikistan**, **Turkmenistan**, where steppe, bazaar, Persian verse, Soviet concrete, and Islam in many temperatures share tea that is a clock.

While if **Armenia** keeps a church so old it looks like the rock decided to pray, stone is a relative. While if **Georgia** raises a glass and sings in harmonies that sound like mountains agreeing, the toast is a theology of guests. While if **Azerbaijan** keeps fire as an old riddle—Zoroastrian memory under later names—fire was never only a weapon. While if **the Kurds**, without one state to hold them, keep Newroz and a language like a portable country, a people can be a country in the mouth.

While if Jews keep Shabbat as a palace in time, time is being sanctified without a square of land having to prove it. While if Christians keep a table of bread and wine, the table is a claim that God eats with the guilty. While if Muslims keep zakat and fasting, the body is being trained toward the poor. While if Hindus keep dharma as a many-pathed duty, duty is not one road. While if Buddhists keep the middle way, the middle is not mediocrity; it is the refusal to be owned by craving. While if Sikhs keep the comb, the steel, the uncut hair as a public honesty, honesty has a uniform. While if Jains sweep the path rather than kill a small life, small life is admitted to the moral world. While if Taoists keep the uncarved block, usefulness includes what is empty. While if Confucian rites keep the dead in the conversation, the conversation is the afterlife that actually affects the living. While if Indigenous ceremonies from the Arctic to the Kalahari to the Amazon to the Outback keep land as kin, kin is the word empires keep trying to translate as resource. While if atheists keep a moral world without a throne in the sky, the keeping is still a faith in the human, and the human is a risky object of faith. While if agnostics keep the question open, openness is a hospitality toward what we do not know.

While if you enter any of these countries as a consumer of difference, you will buy a costume and miss the people. While if you enter as a guest, you will take off the shoes of your certainty. While if you compare religions to see which wins, you have already lost the thing they were trying to protect: a human being unruined by contempt. While if you notice that every people feeds strangers, buries its dead with meaning, marks the year, fears chaos, loves its children, and argues about God, the noticing is the beginning of adult travel. While if borders insist they are eternal, mountains, rivers, recipes, and prayers will continue to smuggle the truth across them. While if a culture is treated as a museum, it will die of glass. While if a culture is treated as a living kitchen, it will feed whoever is willing to wash a dish.

While if you can hold Japan’s bow and Nigeria’s noise, Armenia’s stone and Samoa’s ocean, without ranking them like horses, you will have begun to be a citizen of the actual world. While if you can sit at iftar in Cairo, a Shabbat in Brooklyn, a langar in Amritsar, a tea ceremony in Kyoto, a luau that is not a show, a Greek name-day, a Mexican ofrenda, an Ethiopian coffee roasting, a Sámi joik, a Ukrainian Easter, a Senegalese teranga table, and a quiet atheist vigil for the injured, and while if you can do this without stealing, mocking, or converting the moment into your own monument, then yours is a hospitality large enough to deserve the word human. While if you fail—and you will fail, because no one carries all the names without dropping some—then pick up the next name gently, ask how it prefers to be said, eat what is offered if it is offered, refuse what must be refused without insult, and remember that every country is a cause: because people needed one another in a particular weather, they made a music, a law, a stew, a prayer, a joke, a grave. While if you keep that cause in mind, the map stops being a quiz and becomes a family tree with ten thousand branches, none of which asked your permission to be holy in their own way.
