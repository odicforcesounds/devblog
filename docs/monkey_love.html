Monkey Love

I found some Korean restaurant in the city I live, that have a slogan that say “Eat what You love”.

Then I ask them if they have Dragons, because I love Dragons.

Anyway, this article is not about Dragons, but about monkeys.

I found this cute Monkey that write a lot about stuff I like to explore.

https://s0cm0nkey.gitbook.io/s0cm0nkeys-security-reference-guide/

Is so cute that I almost had an erection! :D

Why a monkey and not a dragon

The dragon is the symbol of power that arrives with a storm and demands respect. The monkey is the symbol of the mind that arrives with a question and steals the answer. One is feared, the other is followed. In the stories I grew up with, the dragon guards the treasure and the monkey learns how to reach it without fighting, which is a much better description of how I work.

So when I say monkey, I am not insulting anyone. I am describing the reader who does not accept the first answer, breaks the rule to see what is behind it, and comes back with a joke instead of a wound.

What that reference guide is

The link above is a collection of notes about security written by another monkey. It is not a course and it is not a product. It is a person who decided to write down what he learned, in the order he learned it, and to leave the notes open for whoever arrives later. That kind of work is worth more than a certificate, because it shows the reasoning and not only the result.

I like it for the same reason I like the ugly documentation: somebody took the time. When a stranger organises a subject for you, the polite thing to do is to read it carefully and then to write your own version with the parts that were missing.

Eat what you love

The restaurant was right, even if they had no dragons. Eat what you love, read what you love, and if what you love does not exist yet, cook it yourself. That is how this blog started, and that is how every reference guide starts: with someone who could not find the page he needed and decided to become the page.
