Just a Funny Perspective that is not false

English from UK is like representing on Theatre.
The More you represent, the better the Sound is. (good for learning)

English from America is like you don't care to represent.
The less you care, the better the Sound is.

Two ways of pronouncing the same word

British English carries the memory of the stage. Vowels are placed with intention, consonants are announced, and the sentence has a shape that a listener can follow from the back of a room. It is a language built to be heard by someone who is far away and cannot ask you to repeat.

American English carries the memory of the street. Sounds collapse, the middle of the word disappears, and the meaning travels in the rhythm instead of the syllables. It is a language built to be efficient for the people who already share the context.

Why one is better for learning

A learner needs the full shape of the word, because he cannot guess the part that was swallowed. That is why the theatrical version is easier to study: everything is there, nothing is assumed, and the mouth of the speaker shows what the ear should expect.

The other version is easier to use once you know the language, and almost impossible to decode before that. Two different tools for two different moments.

The joke at the end

Neither is better, and anyone who says otherwise is defending a passport instead of a language. What changes is the audience the speech was designed for: a theatre full of strangers, or a room full of friends.

I like both, and I use both badly. The important thing is that the other person understands, which is the only standard a language really has.
