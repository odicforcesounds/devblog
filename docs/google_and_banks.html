Google vs Banks

I take some much time to create an app (which is not complete, yet), and Google Payment is against payments that are not banks.

My bank is against most payments that are from online companies, which include Google :)

So, solving another problem with this kind of incompatibilities between giants:

Application will not be in Google Store or Apple Store. ( Free for me and for everyone else)

Binary (.apk) will be stored in Github releases.

Solved.

Two giants, one developer in the middle

The two institutions disagree about who is allowed to move money, and neither of them needs to explain the disagreement to me. Google wants to process the payment inside its own store, the bank wants to process it as a bank transfer or not at all, and the developer is the person who discovers the conflict only after the application is finished.

There is no argument to win here. A rule that exists to protect a business model will not change because a small project is inconvenient, and a bank that distrusts online companies will not change because the company is famous.

What a store really adds

A store adds discovery, an update channel and a payment system. For a free application the first two are nice but not essential, and the third costs a percentage of a price that does not exist. What remains is a signature, a file, and a place where people can find it.

That place can be a release page. The file can be downloaded directly, checked, and installed by whoever decides to trust it. The user loses the convenience of a single button and gains the ability to keep a copy, which in the long term is the same thing as an archive.

The trade I accepted

No store means no payment integration, no commission, no approval queue and no policy change that can remove the application overnight. It also means no visibility, and I accept that too. The people who need the tool will find the repository, and the ones who need a store will find another tool.

Solved, as the text says. Not because the giants agreed, but because the path between them turned out to be open.
