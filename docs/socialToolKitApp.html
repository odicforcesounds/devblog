How it works

The short version of the plan, with what the database actually checks.

One challenge, six moves

  1. 01
    An account

    Google or email signs the person in; the account id is written on every document.

  2. 02
    A group

    The creator leads it, adds members by email and can remove them. Members represent one group at a time.

  3. 03
    A publication

    Any signed-in account offers 5 to 200 coins and a link from YouTube, Vimeo, Facebook, Instagram or TikTok.

  4. 04
    A view

    One per account. The array gains that account id once; a reload cannot add another.

  5. 05
    A proof

    Another group takes it, half its roster marks available, and one member submits the proof link.

  6. 06
    Seven approvals

    People outside the author and the solving group approve or refuse. Seven approvals without a refusal publish it.

What the rules check

WriteCondition
GroupThe leader id is the writer's, the name is 2–60 characters, the timestamp is the server's.
MemberThe writer leads that group; the document id is the group id and the email.
RepresentA membership document exists for that account and group, or the value is empty.
PublishThe author is the writer, the coins are 5–200, views and availability start empty.
TakeThe challenge is open, the writer is not the author, and they represent the solving group.
ViewThe array gains exactly the writer's id, once, and nothing else changes.
RateThe writer is neither the author nor the solving group, and a proof exists.
ResultThe status moves from taken to published and the payout plan is stored with it.

The complete rules live in backups/firebase-rules-stk.txt and must be published in the Firebase console.

What stays outside the page

  • The payout is a plan. Each member's share and the author bonus are computed when the seventh approval arrives and stored on the challenge. Moving coins needs a server with credentials a page must never hold.
  • Seven is counted here. The rules make a rating unique and keep the author and the solving group out, but a rule cannot count a collection. A Cloud Function should own the published status.
  • No upload. A challenge and a proof are links to a source's own player; nothing is copied or rehosted.
  • No moderation or rate limit. A signed-in account can publish repeatedly. App Check and a function that counts writes are the tools for that.

The account is shared with the comments and the temporary chat. Two habits keep the client honest: security is what the server refuses, and validation is repeated in the rules because a check that exists only in JavaScript is a suggestion.
