Blue Team Learning

The Sky is blue and in this site, is only me and you!

Anyway, thank you for attacking my sites and teach me how to block your attacks.

An honest thank you

I mean it. Nobody teaches faster than someone who is trying to get inside without asking. In a few weeks I learned more about my own logs than in a year of reading about hardening, because the attacks told me which door of the house was visible from the street.

The first lesson was that logs are not a record of what happened. They are a record of what somebody else was expecting to find. When the same path is requested a thousand times with three different user agents, that is not a visitor, that is a search.

What this site gave them

Nothing, and that is the point. There are no databases, no users, no sessions and no forms to fill. Everything here is static text, some images and a little music. An attacker who succeeds in reading a page has achieved what a browser achieves by existing.

So the exercise was never about protecting data, it was about protecting attention: my attention, and the patience of a server that should answer readers instead of robots.

What I learned to do

Close what does not need to be open. Answer the scanners with the cheapest possible response. Read the difference between curiosity and automation. And accept that a public service is, by definition, visited by everything that is public, including the parts of the Internet that have no interest in the content at all.

The Sky is blue, and in this site, is only me and you. Everybody else is just passing by with a script and a list.
