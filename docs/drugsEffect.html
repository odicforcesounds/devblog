Drugs are part of a choice

With self discipline, that is able to deny ones desire, drugs can be a good experience, since the consumer will not repeat that experience, over and over again. But drugs are part of reality, part of nature it self, part of each one creativity to mix, doing alchemy. The ability to each one imagine, become bigger, and is associated on how each one feel and believe in what the mind can see, with the ability to imagine, and to react or not to what is happening.

The difference between use and repetition

The experience is not the problem. The repetition is. Anyone can drink a glass of wine and stop there, and anyone can take a substance once and learn something from the change of perception. What turns a choice into a condition is the need to return to it, and the need is not created by the substance alone, it is created by whatever the person was trying to escape.

That is why self discipline is the only thing that matters in this conversation. A person who can say no is free to say yes. A person who cannot refuse is not making a choice at all, he is obeying a chemistry.

Chemistry is not a moral category

A plant does not know what a law is. The molecule exists, it binds to a receptor, it changes the signal. Calling it evil is a human projection, and a useful one for a society that wants to keep the conversation simple, but it explains nothing about the person who takes it.

What explains the person is the state he was in before. Boredom, pain, curiosity, hunger, grief, the wish to understand something that language cannot reach. The substance is an instrument, and instruments are not guilty of the music they are used to play.

What the mind sees when it is altered

The ability to imagine grows when the filter loosens. That is the real effect, more than the pleasure: the mind produces images and connections that were not available before, and some of those images are useful afterwards.

But an image is not a truth. It arrives without proof, and a mind that cannot question what it sees will build a life on a single night. So the rule I keep is simple: enjoy the experience, write down what you learned, and let the discipline decide when, or whether, there is a next time.
