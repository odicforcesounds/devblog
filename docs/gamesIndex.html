The Games Index

A world list of games from the first board to the networked screen: pieces, cards, dice, balls, puzzles, tabletop worlds, arcades, consoles, open-source projects and the games children still teach each other in the street. Every entry carries a short resume, a date or the word traditional, a place, an access label and a link to its own article.

How to read a year. The year is a first record, a publication date or the year a sport was codified — not the day play began. Traditional means the game has no single author and no price; open means the code or rules are free to read, run and change; free means free to play; paid means a commercial copy. The shelves are this catalogue's own grouping, not a claim that one game belongs to one country.

Opening the shelves…

Names, dates, short resumes and thumbnails follow Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons; the shelves, the access labels and the audience labels are this catalogue's own reading. A game that is missing means the shelf has not reached it yet, not that it never existed.
