The comfort of a number

A statistic is a story that arrives already finished. It does not ask for interpretation, it does not hesitate, and it never explains itself. That is why it is so easy to believe: believing costs nothing, while checking costs an evening with the logs.

I believed the number because I wanted the number. Somebody in Hong Kong, somebody in Japan, reading the pages of a small blog written in Portugal. It is a beautiful idea, and a beautiful idea is the easiest thing in the world to accept.

Solipsism, but backwards

Solipsism says that only my mind exists. The reverse would be a world where everything exists except my own judgement, where the evidence is always external and always right. That is the position I fell into: the tool said thousands of visits, so there were thousands of visits, and my own eyes looking at the same logs did not count.

The correction is uncomfortable and useful. The numbers were real, the visitors were not. What the dashboard counted was a crowd of scripts, each one a request, none of them a reader.

What is left after the correction

Not much, and that is the point. A handful of addresses that return on the same days, a few pages opened more than once, a sentence in an email from someone who actually read the text. That is the entire audience of this blog, and it is enough, because those numbers are checkable.

Being wrong is good. It means there is something left to learn, and the lesson here is cheaper than most: trust the log, not the summary.
