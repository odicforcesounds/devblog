More Stories for Children

Two names for the same animal

In every old story the throne has two descriptions. The official one is called the King: order, crown, and the right to speak first. The unofficial one is called the Dragon: the same power, without the permission, sleeping on the gold it was never allowed to spend. Culture writes both because both are true. A community obeys the King, but it learns from the Dragon, because the Dragon is what the King looks like when nobody is watching.

Why culture mixes reality with Fantasy

This is not confusion. When a civilisation mixes reality with fantasy, it is doing something practical: it is putting a thing too big to discuss inside a thing small enough to tell to children. You cannot explain power to a child, but you can explain a Dragon, and the child understands immediately who should not be trusted with the matches.

Stories for children are not only for children

That is why I keep writing them. A good story lets an adult hear the truth while pretending he is only being entertained, and it lets a child carry the truth before he has the words for it. The King and the Dragon will keep changing names, but the shape of the story is always the same, and the children always grow up either to serve one or to become the other.
