The Condition of Illusion

2026-05-24 — —

Since AI told me that my "Educative Game" could offend someone, I wish to explain this cool concept that is real, while this can lead us to create a game with the purpose of educating players to not react in a impulsive way.

So, when you teach, mainly children, they can deny to learn by playing with what reality is suposed to be, without knowing how is it. They will argue with you with many "new theories" trying to deny what you try to teach them, and that create one the teacher condition, to be patience. The level of pacience gets lower, each time the student deny or refuse to learn any lesson.

Since by History, many children learned by the Teachers, using Agressive and Violent feedbacks, like any child can do when is angry with some subject, the purpose of the game is to give the tools to the players, to learn how to deal with the problem of words within our own Imagination, when Paying Attention.

The patience of a teacher is a finite resource

Every person who has taught anything knows the arithmetic. A student who refuses to learn does not only lose his own time, he spends the teacher's. The first refusal costs a sentence. The tenth costs the afternoon. The hundredth costs the teacher, who arrives the next day already tired, and a tired teacher teaches worse, which produces more refusal, which produces more tiredness.

That spiral is where the old violence comes from. Not from cruelty, from exhaustion. A person who has explained the same thing for the twentieth time and hears the same denial begins to believe that the student is doing it on purpose, and from that belief to a slap there is a very short walk. Understanding the mechanism does not excuse the slap, but it explains why it appears in every school, in every country, in every century.

Denial is a stage, not a verdict

A child who denies a lesson is not declaring war on knowledge. He is testing whether the lesson survives contact with his own imagination. He invents a theory that contradicts yours, and he watches what happens: does the adult become angry, or does the adult answer? The answer is the lesson, and the anger is a different lesson, usually the wrong one.

This is why arguing with a child is not a failure of authority. It is the classroom. The adult's job is not to win, it is to remain a person while losing the argument, because the child is learning from the behaviour far more than from the content.

Why a game, and not a sermon

A sermon teaches a rule. A game teaches a reflex. If the goal is that a person does not react impulsively when a word hurts him, no amount of explanation will do it, because explanation arrives after the reaction. The reaction has to be trained before it, in a place where the cost of failing is a point lost instead of a friendship broken.

That is the whole design of the idea: put the player in situations where the first answer is anger, and let the game reward the second answer. Not by shaming the first one, but by showing, in points, in narrative, in the face of a character, what the second answer produces.

A sketch of the game

Nothing here is code yet, only rules. A player meets a creature that says something offensive about him. The interface offers four answers: attack, deny, ignore, ask. Attacking costs energy and escalates. Denying loops the scene forever. Ignoring works but teaches nothing. Asking opens a branch where the creature explains its own fear, and the player discovers that the insult was never about him.

Each level raises the cost of the first impulse and lowers the reward of the second. By the end, the winning move is the one that takes a breath. If the player learns that with a keyboard, the chance of remembering it in a kitchen argument is not zero, and a chance that is not zero is the only thing an educator can honestly promise.

What the monster is not

The monster is not the child, and it is not the student. The monster is the behaviour that appears when a person is afraid and has no words for the fear. Since we all have that behaviour somewhere, the game is not about correcting others, it is about recognising the moment when the creature appears in our own voice, and choosing the second answer.

That is why the title says without becoming a monster. The teacher who loses patience becomes the thing he was trying to explain, and the lesson ends there. The only way out is the one the game trains: breathe, look at the person, and answer the fear instead of the insult.
