The Beauty of the Ugly

Based on the Arch Linux review, I need to tell you.

For years, the Ugly is and was, the best source of information!

Awesome Documentation!!!

Ugly is a compliment here

When I say Ugly I am not talking about the quality of the work. I am talking about the shape of it: the page with no design, the plain text file, the manual that was written by the person who actually wrote the code, in a language that is not always polite and in a format that no marketing department would ever approve.

That is exactly why it works. An ugly document has nothing to sell. There is no landing page, no carousel of features, no button asking you to subscribe before you can read how the program behaves when the disk is full. Someone had a problem, someone solved it, and then wrote down what happened. The reader is treated as a person who wants to understand, not as a person who wants to be convinced.

Beauty does not compile

I have lost more time to beautiful documentation than to ugly documentation. The beautiful one is usually written by someone reading the code for the first time, describing what the code appears to do. The ugly one is written by the hand that fought with the bug at three in the morning. One explains the intention, the other explains the behaviour, and behaviour is the only thing a machine obeys.

This is the same reason why the best answers in a forum thread are often the shortest, the most badly formatted, and the most useful. Somebody tested it. Somebody paid for that knowledge with their own time, and decided that the next person should not pay again.

What I look for before I install anything

First the manual page, then the mailing list archive, then the issue tracker. If those three exist and the people inside them speak plainly about what is broken, the software is probably good. If everything is beautiful and nothing mentions a limitation, the software is probably young.

The Arch Linux review that started this text is the same story: the distribution is famous for its documentation, and the documentation is famous for being a wall of text. A wall of text that answers the question, which is more than most polished sites can do.
