Thesis

All rivers flow from one spring

Philosophy is not one subject among others. It is the mother discipline — the practice of asking foundational questions, defining concepts, testing reasons and building systems. Every modern profession below broke away from philosophy, yet still answers to it.


⚖

Laws & Justice

From Hammurabi’s stele to Solon, Cicero’s natural law, Al-Farabi, Aquinas, Grotius, Locke, Montesquieu and Rawls — the very idea that law must be justified, not merely obeyed, is philosophical. Jurisprudence = ethics + logic applied to power.

Society & Politics

Plato’s Republic, Confucius’ rites, Ibn Khaldun’s sociology, Hobbes, Rousseau’s social contract, Wollstonecraft, Gandhi, Arendt — every constitution, parliament and revolution began as a philosophical argument about the good city.

Polis · Civitas · Umma

Science & Logic

Thales, Democritus’ atoms, Aristotle’s logic, Nyaya inference, Alhazen’s optics, Bacon’s method, Descartes, Popper’s falsification, Kuhn’s paradigms. Science is natural philosophy grown up — it still borrows causality, proof and truth from philosophy.

Why it matters

Practical benefits of thinking deeply

Philosophy is often mocked as useless. Yet employers, doctors, judges and engineers pay for exactly what it teaches: clarity under confusion.


01

Clearer reasoning

Spot fallacies, separate premises from conclusions, weigh evidence. Studies link philosophy training to top scores in logic-heavy exams and better debugging.

02

Ethical judgment

Trolley problems become triage protocols, AI guardrails and courtroom reasoning. You learn to decide when values collide — which is every important decision.

03

Democratic resilience

Socrates taught Athens to question demagogues. Media literacy, tolerance and the ability to steelman opponents are civic self-defense.

04

Inner steadiness

Stoicism, Buddhism, Epicureanism and Sufism are ancient cognitive therapies: distinguish what you control, accept impermanence, desire less, attend fully.

05

Scientific creativity

Einstein read Hume and Mach; Heisenberg read Plato; Darwin read Malthus and Herschel. Paradigm shifts need conceptual courage — philosophy’s specialty.

06

Cross-cultural wisdom

Reading Confucius, Al-Ghazali, Shankara, Ife bronzes’ worldview and Quechua reciprocity (ayni) dissolves provincialism and enriches every profession.

07

Meaning & mortality

Epicurus, Montaigne, Simone de Beauvoir, Viktor Frankl: philosophy does not remove suffering, but gives it language, proportion and purpose.

08

Better conversation

The Socratic method — ask, clarify, test, revise — turns debates into joint inquiries. Teams, marriages and parliaments all run on it.

3000 BCE — Today

A world journey of thought

Follow the idea of wisdom around the planet. Each era below expands — open them to meet thinkers, texts and breakthroughs from every continent.

1. First Questions — Mesopotamia & Egypt

c. 3000 – 800 BCE · Iraq · Egypt

Writing itself is a philosophical invention: to fix law and memory outside the mind. The Epic of Gilgamesh asks the first recorded existential question — how to face death. Egypt’s Ptahhotep (c. 2400 BCE) teaches humility and just speech; Maat makes cosmic order the basis of justice.

GilgameshMaxims of PtahhotepCode of HammurabiMaat
Read deeper

Hammurabi’s Code (c. 1754 BCE) is more than punishment — its prologue claims the king was chosen “to prevent the strong from oppressing the weak.” That justification of power by justice is political philosophy in stone, a thousand years before Plato.

2. India — Upanishads, Buddha, Logic

c. 800 BCE – 500 CE · India · Nepal

The Upanishads discover Atman = Brahman: the self and the absolute are one. The Buddha (c. 5th c. BCE) responds with the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path — suffering can be diagnosed and ended. Mahavira founds Jain non-violence; Panini writes a formal grammar that anticipates computer science; the Nyaya school builds a full theory of inference and debate.

  • Texts: Brihadaranyaka & Chandogya Upanishads, Dhammapada, Yoga Sutras, Arthashastra of Kautilya
  • Discoveries: zero & decimal thought, atomism (Vaisheshika), hospitals, inoculation debates, linguistic formalism
VedantaBuddhismJainismNyaya logicCharaka & Sushruta medicine

3. China — A Hundred Schools Contend

c. 600 – 200 BCE · China

Confucius makes ethics a social architecture: ren (humaneness), li (rites), filial piety and merit over blood. Laozi answers with the Daodejing: act by wu-wei, effortless alignment with the Tao. Mozi preaches universal love and early logic; Legalists design the bureaucratic state; Sun Tzu turns war into strategy.

  • Texts: Analects, Daodejing, Zhuangzi, Mencius, Xunzi, Art of War
  • Discoveries: civil examinations, paper, compass ethics of governance, yin-yang systems thinking
ConfucianismTaoismMohismLegalism

4. Persia, Greece & the Mediterranean Dawn

c. 600 – 300 BCE · Iran · Greece · Turkey

Zoroaster frames history as cosmic choice between truth and lie — an idea that shapes Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Thales predicts an eclipse and seeks a single arche; Heraclitus finds flux, Parmenides being, Democritus atoms. Then Socrates turns philosophy to the examined life, Plato founds the Academy and the theory of Forms, Aristotle invents logic, biology, poetics and political science.

  • Texts: Gathas, Presocratic fragments, Apology, Republic, Nicomachean Ethics, Metaphysics
  • Discoveries: deductive proof, atomism, syllogism, empirical biology, democracy critiqued and defended
ZoroastrianismSocratic methodAcademy & Lyceum

5. Hellenistic, Roman & African Wisdom

c. 300 BCE – 400 CE · Rome · Alexandria · Africa

Epicurus cures fear of gods and death; Zeno’s Stoics teach cosmopolitan duty and control of assent — later the operating system of Roman emperors like Marcus Aurelius. Cicero and Seneca Latinize philosophy into law and conscience. Meanwhile Hypatia of Alexandria teaches mathematics, Augustine (born in Algeria) fuses Platonism with Christianity, and Kushite, Aksumite and Yoruba cosmologies sustain rich oral philosophies of destiny and community.

StoicismEpicureanismSkepticismRoman lawAlexandria

6. The Golden Ages — Baghdad, Córdoba, Nalanda, Chang’an

c. 700 – 1300 · Middle East · Spain · India · China · Mali

While Europe fragments, philosophy goes global. The House of Wisdom translates Plato, Aristotle and Indian mathematics into Arabic. Al-Kindi, Al-Farabi, Avicenna (Ibn Sina), Averroes (Ibn Rushd), Al-Ghazali, Maimonides debate reason vs. revelation, prove God’s existence, and preserve medicine. Alhazen (Ibn al-Haytham) founds experimental optics. Nalanda hosts 10,000 debating monks; Nagarjuna’s emptiness logic reshapes Asia; Zhu Xi systematizes Neo-Confucianism; Timbuktu manuscripts record African astronomy and law.

  • Texts: Avicenna’s Canon & Healing, Averroes’ Commentaries, Al-Ghazali’s Incoherence, Maimonides’ Guide, Nagarjuna’s Middle Way
  • Discoveries: algebra, algorithms (Al-Khwarizmi), optics & scientific method, hospitals, universities
Islamic philosophyNalandaNeo-ConfucianismTimbuktu

7. Americas & Oceania Before 1492

Before 1500 · Mexico · Peru · North America · Pacific

Philosophy was never Eurasia-only. Mexica tlamatinime (“knowers of things”) composed Nahua poetry asking if life is dream (“Do we truly live on earth?” — Nezahualcoyotl). Maya scribes track time cyclically; Inca ayni reciprocity organizes economy as ethics; Haudenosaunee Great Law of Peace models federal democracy; Aboriginal Dreaming encodes law, ecology and metaphysics in songlines.

Nahua thoughtMaya cosmologyAndean reciprocityDreaming

8. Late Medieval to Renaissance — Faith Meets Reason

1200 – 1600 · Europe · Ethiopia · Japan

Aquinas harmonizes Aristotle with faith; Ockham’s razor trims metaphysics; Christine de Pizan defends women’s reason; Ethiopian Zera Yacob (later) pioneers rational critique. Dogen founds Soto Zen in Japan; Rumi turns Sufism into universal love poetry. Montaigne invents the essay — “What do I know?” — and Erasmus makes tolerance a program.

ScholasticismZenSufismHumanism

9. Scientific Revolution & Enlightenment

1600 – 1800 · Europe · Americas

Descartes (“I think, therefore I am”), Bacon’s method, Spinoza’s God-nature, Locke’s rights, Hume’s skepticism, Kant’s Copernican turn, Voltaire and Montesquieu’s separation of powers. Across the Atlantic, Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz defends women’s intellect; Haitian revolutionaries turn Rousseau against slavery; Newton still calls himself a natural philosopher.

  • Texts: Meditations, Essay Concerning Human Understanding, Critique of Pure Reason, Social Contract, Vindication of the Rights of Woman (Wollstonecraft, 1792)
  • Discoveries: calculus debate, physics, rights of man, abolitionism, modern university
RationalismEmpiricismSocial contractRights

10. Modern Upheavals — Industry, Colony, Freedom

1800 – 1950 · Worldwide

Hegel and Marx historicize everything; Mill defends liberty and utilitarianism; Nietzsche declares God dead and values revisable; Freud maps the unconscious. Anti-colonial philosophy erupts: Swami Vivekananda, Tagore, Gandhi’s satya & ahimsa; Fanon, Nkrumah, Senghor’s Negritude; José Martí, Mariátegui; Sun Yat-sen; Kitaro Nishida’s Kyoto School fusing Zen and phenomenology.

IdealismMarxismExistentialismDecolonial thoughtKyoto School

11. Contemporary — Logic, Justice, Planet, Machine

1950 – Today · Global

Russell, Wittgenstein, Gödel remake logic; Arendt theorizes totalitarianism; Rawls revives justice as fairness; Beauvoir, Butler, bell hooks, Oyěwùmí remake gender; Sen & Nussbaum remake development as capability; Singer, Jonas, Latour remake our duties to animals and climate; Turing, Wiener, Floridi found AI ethics. Ubuntu, Buen Vivir and Confucian ecology enter constitutions (Ecuador, Bolivia) and UN debates.

  • Texts: Second Sex, Theory of Justice, Capabilities Approach, Silent Spring’s philosophical heirs, Superintelligence debates
  • Discoveries: computers, internet, human rights law, bioethics, climate ethics, AI alignment

Fruits

Great discoveries philosophy gave birth to

Each of these began as a philosophical speculation before becoming a laboratory result or an institution.


Ϝ�

Atomism → Physics & Chemistry

Democritus and Kanada’s Vaisheshika argued reality is indivisible particles in void — 2,300 years before Dalton, Brownian motion and particle colliders confirmed them.

∞

Logic → Mathematics & Computing

Aristotle’s syllogism + Nyaya inference + Frege/Russell + Gödel + Turing = Boolean circuits, programming languages and AI. Panini’s grammar prefigured formal language theory.

⚖

Natural law → Human rights

Stoics → Cicero → Aquinas → Grotius → Locke → 1948 Universal Declaration. The claim that dignity limits power is pure philosophy, now international law.

🔬

Empiricism → Experimental science

Alhazen’s doubt, Bacon’s tables, Hume’s problem of induction, Popper’s falsification: the lab’s rulebook was written by philosophers.

✚

Ethics → Modern medicine

Hippocratic oath, Avicenna’s Canon, informed consent, research ethics boards, triage and public-health justice — every hospital runs on moral philosophy.

🌐

Cosmopolitanism → Global civism

Diogenes “citizen of the world”, Kant’s perpetual peace, Ubuntu, Buen Vivir → United Nations, climate treaties, refugee law and digital citizenship debates.

Compare

Major schools, side by side

Click a tab. Notice how different civilizations independently converged on the same big answers — and the same big disagreements.

Greece — Stoicism & Epicureanism: control assent, want little, serve the cosmopolis; or maximize tranquil pleasure, fear nothing.

China — Confucianism vs. Taoism: cultivate virtue through rites and relations; or unlearn striving and flow with Tao.

India — Buddha & Gita: end craving via the Eightfold Path; or act selflessly (karma-yoga) without clinging to fruits.

Africa — Ubuntu: personhood is co-created — “I am because we are.” Ethics is belonging made responsible.

Modern — Kant vs. Utilitarianism: never use persons as mere means; or maximize total wellbeing. Hospitals and courts use both daily.

Plato vs. Aristotle: eternal Forms vs. forms in things — the original rationalism/empiricism split.

Advaita vs. Buddhism: one Self (Shankara) vs. no-self and dependent arising (Nagarjuna) — opposite cures for the same suffering.

Zhuangzi & Descartes: butterfly dream doubts the self playfully; the cogito doubts everything to find certainty.

Yoruba, Akan & Nahua: destiny, vital force and “flower-and-song” aesthetics — personhood as balance of body, soul and community.

Today: phenomenology, cognitive science and Kyoto School “nothingness” jointly probe consciousness and AI minds.

Plato, Kautilya, Han Fei: philosopher-kings, the realistic Arthashastra state, Legalist bureaucracy — order through wisdom, interest or law.

Ibn Khaldun: asabiyyah (social cohesion) drives the rise and fall of dynasties — sociology 400 years early.

Locke, Rousseau, Wollstonecraft: government by consent; general will; no consent without women’s rights.

Gandhi, Fanon, Rawls: nonviolent swaraj, decolonial liberation, justice as fairness behind a veil of ignorance.

Living proof: constitutions from South Africa (Ubuntu) to Ecuador (rights of nature) cite philosophy verbatim.

Nyaya & Aristotle: four means of knowledge (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) meet the syllogism.

Alhazen & Bacon: controlled experiment replaces authority — “the seeker after truth must doubt.”

Hume, Kant, Popper, Kuhn: induction has no proof; mind shapes experience; science grows by falsification and revolutions.

Mohist & Mayan astronomy: optics, eclipses and calendars show science was always multicultural.
