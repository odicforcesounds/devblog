    L0

    L0 — What “capitalism” technically is

    PREREQ: NONE · OBJECTIVE: DEFINE THE 5 PILLARS · TIME 15 MIN

    0.1 Definition without slogans

    Capitalism is a coordination system where (1) most productive assets are privately owned, (2) production is guided by prices formed in markets, (3) investment comes from accumulated capital seeking return, (4) labour is hired via wage contracts, and (5) firms compete under (imperfect) rule of law. Remove any pillar and you get a different system (see L6).

    Capital
    Wealth set aside to produce more wealth: machines, stock, cash, code, reputation.
    Market
    Any protocol where buyers/sellers meet and a price clears (bazaar, NYSE, eBay, Uber surge).
    Profit
    Revenue − full costs. Signal that says “do more of this” (or “stop”).
    Competition
    Rivalry that copies success and punishes waste — when entry is actually open.

    0.2 Animated: the circular flow

    FIG 0-A · HOUSEHOLDS ⇄ FIRMS (ANIMATED)
    Households → labour → Firms → wages → Households → spending → Firms …

    Households sell labour/capital; firms pay wages/interest/dividends; households buy goods; money loops. Leaks: savings, taxes, imports. Injections: investment, state spending, exports.

    0.3 Why prices matter (the information argument)

    Economist F. Hayek’s point: no planner knows everyone’s needs. A rising bread price compresses thousands of facts (bad harvest + fuel cost + demand) into one number everyone can act on. That is powerful — and also brutal, because price alone ignores who can pay.

    L1

    L1 — How production works: recipe + machine + organisation

    PREREQ: L0 · PRODUCTION FUNCTION · DIVISION OF LABOUR · TIME 20 MIN

    1.1 The recipe: land, labour, capital, knowledge

    Output = f(land, labour, capital, know-how). Capitalism’s bet: if people can keep part of the surplus, they will reinvest in better recipes (technology) and machines. Example: a bakery. Same flour + hands, but a €4,000 oven doubles loaves per hour — that oven is capital.

    1.2 Division of labour (Smith’s pin factory, real numbers)

    Smith observed ~10 workers making 48,000 pins/day by splitting 18 operations vs. ~200 pins if each worked alone. Modern twin: Amazon fulfilment centres or Foxconn lines for Apple — extreme splitting raises throughput but can deskill work. That trade-off is technical, not ideological.

    L2 — Markets & prices: supply, demand, and when they lie

    PREREQ: L1 · EQUILIBRIUM · ELASTICITY · EXTERNALITIES · TIME 25 MIN

    2.1 How a price is born

    Demand slopes down (cheaper → more wanted), supply slopes up (pricier → more offered). Where they cross = equilibrium. Move either curve and price + quantity adjust. No mysticism.

    FIG 2-A · LIVE SUPPLY / DEMAND — DRAG THE SHIFTERS
    P*=110 · Q*=150

    Try: heatwave → demand right (price up). New factory → supply right (price down). Real twin: Uber surge pricing and Airbnb seasonal pricing.

    L3 — Capital & finance: interest, shares, risk

    PREREQ: L2 · COMPOUNDING · STOCKS/BONDS · BANKS · TIME 25 MIN

    3.1 Why interest exists

    €100 today > €100 next year (you could use it, and the future is uncertain). Interest is the price of time + risk. Compounding = interest earning interest — the engine of both fortunes and debt traps.

    L4

    L4 — Labour, wages & the firm: why companies exist

    PREREQ: L3 · WAGES · ENTREPRENEURSHIP · COOPERATIVES · TIME 20 MIN

    4.1 Why wages differ

    Wages ≈ supply of a skill vs demand for it, plus bargaining power, plus rents (licences, networks, location). A nurse and a Solidity dev differ less by “merit” than by scarcity + ability to capture value. Minimum wages, unions, and monopsony (one big employer, e.g. a single factory town or a dominant delivery platform) shift the split.

    4.2 Why firms exist at all (Coase, 1937)

    Why not hire a freelancer for every micro-task? Because transaction costs (finding, contracting, supervising) are real. Firms replace endless bargaining with hierarchy — until hierarchy itself becomes the cost (bureaucracy). Platforms like Upwork / Fiverr / Uber push the boundary back toward markets by lowering search costs.

    4.3 Entrepreneurship vs rent-seeking

    ActivityCreates value?Example
    Productive entrepreneurshipYes — new/better/cheaperBioNTech mRNA work; Shopify enabling SMEs; M-Pesa mobile money (Kenya)
    Rent-seekingNo — captures existing pieLobbying for a monopoly licence; patent trolling; rigged privatisation (Russia 1990s — guilty: oligarchs + officials who designed loans-for-shares)
    Cooperative / mutualYes — shared ownershipMondragón (Spain), Crédit Mutuel, John Lewis Partnership — capitalist ownership, democratic governance

    Enforces contracts/property, issues money, taxes, provides public goods, regulates externalities, redistributes, and bails out (or doesn’t). Every success and disaster in this manual involves this actor — neutral description, then judgment with names.

    6.2 Parties are not systems — mapping the words

    “Communist”, “socialist”, “social-democratic”, “liberal”, “conservative”, “libertarian” mean different things per country. Rule of thumb: ask who owns what, who decides prices, who can be fired by voters. Examples: Sweden’s Social Democrats govern a deeply capitalist export economy with high taxes; China’s Communist Party governs a market economy without political competition; US Republicans and Democrats both govern capitalism but differ on tax/regulation/welfare; France’s RN vs LFI differ on identity + state but both accept mixed economy.

    Method note: GDP per capita (PPP), Gini, HDI, and World Bank poverty series are the shared scoreboard. They flatter markets and miss care work, nature, and fear. Read them together, never alone.

    8.2 Boom-bust anatomy (Minsky in 5 steps)

    Displacement (new tech) → Boom (credit) → Euphoria (prices detach) → Profit-taking (smart money exits) → Panic (leverage unwinds). Platforms amplify it: Robinhood/GameStop 2021, crypto leverage, housing portals. Euphoria is measurable: price-to-rent, CAPE ratio, loan-to-income.

    L9 — New ideas for peaceful, useful societies (build, don’t preach)

    PREREQ: L8 · DESIGN PATTERNS · LOCAL PLAYBOOK · FINAL EXAM · TIME 30 MIN

    9.1 Design constraints (engineering, not utopia)

    Any proposal must pass: (a) incentive-compatible (works even if people are selfish), (b) falsifiable (metrics + sunset clause), (c) power-constrained (losers can reverse it), (d) funded (who pays, in numbers). If it fails one, it’s a wish.

    9.3 How to produce (a 90-day local playbook)

    Days 1–15: map one street’s unused capacity (rooms on Airbnb-style listing? tools? skills?). 16–45: form a co-op or micro-firm on Shopify/eBay/Etsy with 3 products and real unit economics from L1. 46–75: add one commons (repair slot, shared printer, tutoring). 76–90: publish accounts + vote next cycle (pattern 5). Measure: revenue, hours, waste avoided, neighbours served. If numbers fail, pivot — that’s capitalism’s useful half (selection), tamed by the peaceful half (no one starves during the test).
