A Letter From A Simple Human To Leaders: Please Stop The War — Please Stop All Wars

From: a person with no title, no army, no office. Just eyes, and a heart that is tired.
To: leaders in Israel, in Palestine, in every capital where orders are signed.
Date: today, while bombs still fall and hostages still wait and mothers still do not sleep.

Dear leaders,

My name does not matter. I am not important. I fix things, I cook, I wait in lines, I pay bills late sometimes. I have neighbors who pray on Friday, neighbors who pray on Saturday, neighbors who do not pray at all. We share sugar. That is my credential: I share sugar.

I am writing because I cannot sleep while children are being buried. I cannot say Israeli children or Palestinian children as if one hurts more. A child is a child. When I see a small shoe in rubble in Gaza, I think of my own kid's shoes by the door. When I see a mother in Tel Aviv waiting for a hostage son who never comes home, I think of my mother waiting for me when I was late. Pain does not have a flag. Fear does not have a religion. Hunger does not check ID.

I know you will say it is complicated. I know history is long and blood is old. I have read enough to know: Holocaust and Nakba, wars in 48, 67, 73, intifadas, rockets, blockades, settlements, tunnels, October 7th and everything after. I am not naive. I know Hamas did monstrous things on October 7th — killing families, taking hostages, raping, burning. That was evil. I condemn it with my whole body. I know Israel has a right to live in safety, that Jewish people need safety after centuries of persecution. I know Palestinians have lived under occupation and blockade, humiliated at checkpoints, losing homes, losing olive trees older than states. That is also unbearable. Two truths can bleed at once.

But I am a simple human and I ask a simple question: how many dead children will make you safe? Give me a number. 1,000? 10,000? 15,000? At what number do you stop and say, now we are secure? Because we passed numbers that should have stopped any hand. In Gaza, entire families erased from civil registries. No one left to mourn them because mourners also dead. Hospitals without power, doctors operating by phone light, babies in incubators dying when fuel runs out. In Israel, families torn, kibbutzim burned, 200+ hostages taken, survivors of a music festival running through fields. Trauma that will last generations. And now Lebanon bleeding too, Ukraine bleeding for years, Sudan starving in silence, Myanmar, Sahel. The world is a ward of bleeding rooms and you hold the keys.

I watch videos I wish I could unsee. A father in Gaza holding a plastic bag with pieces of his son, saying this is my son. A grandmother in Israel lighting candles for grandchildren murdered. A boy in Rafah carrying his sister's body because no stretcher. A father in Sderot running to shelter with kids in 15 seconds. Different languages, same scream. I am not a leader, but I know: if your strategy creates endless videos of screams, your strategy failed, even if generals call it progress.

You say you must destroy Hamas. I understand rage after massacre. But tell me honestly: can you bomb an idea? Every bomb that kills a child creates ten new angers. Every house flattened recruits. Israel's own history proves: occupation and humiliation do not produce safety. Palestine's tragedy proves: rockets and massacres do not produce freedom. Both paths tried for decades. Both failed. More of failed is not strength. It is addiction.

You say you must defend Israel's existence. I agree Israel must exist, safe, recognized. But safety cannot be built on mass graves of neighbors. A wall, a blockade, a siege — they keep bodies out, not hatred out. True security is when a Palestinian mother in Gaza wants her son to be a doctor, not a martyr, because she believes he can live. True freedom is when an Israeli mother in Ashkelon lets her kids play outside without listening for sirens. Those mothers want the same thing. Why do you give them opposite things?

I am not writing from a palace. I wake at 6, make coffee, worry about rent. I know what it means to be tired and still go on. So I imagine you are tired too. War is exhausting even for those who order it. So let me speak tired to tired: stop. Not pause for cameras. Stop.

What I beg is concrete, not poetic. I beg for an immediate ceasefire in Gaza and Israel, release of all hostages now, flood of aid now, no more bombs on tents, no more rockets on towns. I beg for protection of hospitals, schools, bakeries, water plants — no excuses, no "but they hide there." If they hide there, you do not get to kill the patients to get them. Find another way. You have drones, intelligence, special forces. Use precision of law, not weight of bombs.

I beg for the West Bank: stop settler violence, stop demolitions, stop night raids that make teenagers into fighters. I beg for Lebanon: stop escalation before another country collapses. I beg for Ukraine, Sudan, all forgotten wars: same rule — civilians are not targets, not collateral, not messages. They are people.

You will say: they will not stop, so we cannot stop. Someone must be first. History remembers who was first to stop. Egypt and Israel were enemies until Sadat flew to Jerusalem. Northern Ireland seemed impossible until enemies sat. Apartheid seemed eternal until it ended. First is lonely. First is leadership. Be first.

Let me tell you what war looks like from below, because you see maps with arrows and I see kitchens.

War from below is a mother boiling dirty water and telling kids it is soup. War is a father writing children's names on their legs in marker so bodies can be identified. Have you ever written your child's name on their leg for death? I write mine on lunchboxes for school. The difference between us is not love. It is luck and your decisions.

War is a nurse in Gaza doing 48-hour shifts, choosing which child gets oxygen. War is a teacher in Israel learning to barricade a classroom with tables in 20 seconds. War is a baker in Deir al-Balah baking with flour mixed with sand because no flour. War is a farmer who cannot reach olives because of soldiers or rockets. War is a fisherman who cannot fish because sea is blockade. War is a teenage girl who stopped dreaming of university and dreams of water.

And war is lies we tell to survive it. We say "surgical strikes" when surgery heals and strikes kill. We say "human shields" to make dead families sound like tactics. We say "evacuate" when there is nowhere safe, no fuel, no wheelchairs, no time. We say "never again" while doing again to others. Simple humans hear these words and feel sick, because we know language is being used to wash blood.

I do not hate you. I need you to know that. I do not think you are monsters. I think you are humans trapped in machines that reward toughness and punish mercy. Advisors tell you ceasefire is weakness. Polls tell you revenge is popular. Arms dealers tell you stock is ready. History tells you compromise is betrayal. I understand pressure. But pressure is not permission to kill children. If a father under pressure killed a neighbor's child, we would jail him. Why does office change murder into policy?

Let me go deeper, because easy peace slogans do not stop tanks. I want to answer your objections as a simple human who has listened to all sides until ears bled.

You say: "We must eliminate Hamas to protect Israelis." I hear fear of another October 7th. That fear is real. No Israeli should live fearing paragliders with guns at dawn. But ask your own generals quietly: can Hamas be eliminated by bombs? Hamas is not only tunnels; it is also anger at blockade, at occupation, at humiliation. You can kill leaders, you cannot kill humiliation with missiles. Every university destroyed, every journalist killed, every aid convoy turned back teaches a 10-year-old in Gaza that the world allows his erasure. That 10-year-old in 10 years will be what you claim to fight now. The only way to eliminate Hamas as idea is to eliminate despair that feeds it: freedom of movement, work, dignity, state. Security through strangulation is illusion. Ask anyone who lived under siege: siege does not make people docile. It makes them desperate.

You say: "Palestinians teach hate, celebrate murder." Some do. Some Israelis celebrate bombing Gaza, dance at weddings singing death to villages. Hate exists both ways, fed by blood. But most Palestinians I see in videos just want to live: a teacher protecting students under stairs, a dentist pulling teeth by flashlight, a boy sharing bread with a cat in rubble. Most Israelis just want to live: a peace activist who drove Gazan kids to hospitals before Oct 7 and was killed that day, hostage families begging for deal, soldiers' mothers begging not to send sons to die in tunnels for revenge that never ends. Do not let extremists define peoples. Extremists need war to stay important. Ordinary people need peace to stay alive. Choose ordinary.

You say: "There is no partner for peace." Then be partner for law. Even without love, there is law: Geneva Conventions, prohibition of collective punishment, of starvation as weapon, of targeting civilians, of hostage-taking. Both sides violated. Hamas violated by massacring civilians and taking hostages. Israel violates by bombing dense neighborhoods, cutting water and power, displacing 2 million, killing aid workers and journalists at rates unseen. I am not a court, but I know: if only Hamas is condemned and state bombing excused as self-defense, law dies. Law must bind the strong most, because strong can kill most. Uphold law even when enemy breaks it. That is what makes you different from them.

You say: "Civilians die because Hamas hides among them." I ask: where else can they go in one of densest places on earth, locked by blockade? Gaza is 365 square km with 2.3 million people. Half children. There is no empty battlefield. There are apartments, schools, hospitals. If enemy hides among civilians, you still may not massacre civilians. A bank robber in a kindergarten does not allow bombing kindergarten. Police surround, negotiate, risk themselves to save kids. Why is war allowed lower morality than policing? If you cannot fight without killing thousands of kids, you cannot claim precision. You must stop, change method, accept risk to soldiers rather than transfer all risk to children. Soldiers volunteered; children did not.

You say: "We warn with leaflets and calls." Warnings to go where? South, then south bombed. "Safe zones" bombed. Families carrying white flags shot. Disabled, elderly, ICU babies cannot evacuate. Warning does not make killing lawful when no safe place exists and when you destroy the means of life after. A warning before destroying water, food, medicine is not humanity. It is theater.

You say: "Aid is entering." A trickle while famine spreads is not aid. It is alibi. 500 trucks a day needed, 50 allowed, looted in chaos you created by destroying police and order. Shooting starving people at food lines, as happened, is horror. Let UN, Red Crescent, journalists move freely. Stop vetoing. Hunger as pressure is war crime. A child dying of hunger while flour waits at border is indictment of all of us.

You say: "Hostages must be freed by force." Hostage families themselves plead for deal, not bombs. Bombs killed hostages. Deals freed them. Every ex-general knows: you do not bomb hostages to save them. You negotiate, exchange, stop fire. Release all hostages now — Hamas must, unconditionally — and stop bombing to let them live. Link them: ceasefire frees living, recovers dead, ends torture of waiting.

You say: "The world hates us / the world ignores us." Both peoples feel alone. Israelis feel world excuses Oct 7. Palestinians feel world excuses Gaza destruction. Both partly right. Antisemitism is real and rising; Islamophobia and anti-Palestinian racism real too. Do not compete in victimhood. Grief is not zero-sum. Acknowledge both: Oct 7 was pogrom-like massacre; Gaza bombing is catastrophic collective punishment. Say both sentences. Leaders who can say both can lead to peace. Leaders who say only one lead to endless war.

Now to Palestinian leaders, Hamas and others: I speak bluntly as human, not diplomat. Release hostages now. Stop firing rockets at towns. Stop using schools and hospitals for war. Stop stealing aid. Stop executing dissenters. Stop promising to repeat Oct 7. That promise murders your own people's future. You claim resistance, but what did Oct 7 bring Palestinians except rubble and death? True resistance is building life under oppression without becoming oppressors of your own: schools, clinics, nonviolent mass movement, unity, diplomacy. Apartheid South Africa fell not by massacring white families but by making oppression unsustainable through struggle, strikes, boycotts, and moral clarity. Be moral to be unbeatable. Cruelty makes you lose even when you claim victory.

To Arab and Muslim leaders, to Iran, to Hezbollah, to Houthis: stop using Palestinians as chess pieces. Speeches while doing little, or firing rockets that bring retaliation on civilians, is not solidarity. It is performance. Open borders for aid, take wounded, fund rebuilding, press for ceasefire, stop escalation that turns Gaza war into regional hell. Lebanon cannot bear another ruin. Do not add bodies to prove loyalty.

To American, European, all Western leaders: your weapons, money, vetoes enable. You cannot send 2,000-pound bombs and then express concern at funerals. You cannot call for restraint while blocking ceasefire resolutions. Restraint without consequences is permission. Condition aid on compliance with law. Stop settlements expansion you call illegal but fund indirectly. Sanction settler violence. Protect journalists you claim to cherish. Consistent law or no credibility. Young people in your streets see double standards and lose faith in democracy you preach.

To all: the day after cannot be more occupation, more blockade, more tunnels. Day after must be political horizon: end occupation, two states living side by side in 1967 borders with swaps, Jerusalem shared, refugees addressed justly, security guarantees for both, equal rights. If two states killed by settlements, then equal rights in one shared land. But not apartheid, not open prison, not theocracy. People equal. I am simple: either divide land fairly or share land fairly. What is unfair is current: one people controls, other is controlled.

I want to speak about all wars now, because Gaza bleeds brightest on screens but not alone. Ukraine: two years of trenches, villages erased, children deported, soldiers ground like wheat. I tell Russian and Ukrainian leaders same as Middle East: no land is worth this mountain of corpses. Negotiate, withdraw, guarantee, rebuild. Sudan: millions displaced, hunger used, world looks away because cameras few. I beg: open aid, stop arming militias, stop rape as weapon. Myanmar, Sahel, Congo, Haiti gangs: same plea. Everywhere rule is same: do not kill civilians to keep power. Power built on graves collapses into graves.

Why do wars continue when peoples want peace? Polls show majorities in Israel and Palestine want deal, want quiet. Ukrainians want homes, Russians mothers want sons. Sudanese want bread. So why bombs? Because war benefits few: arms firms profit, extremist politicians survive by fear, media profits by rage, foreign powers test weapons. Simple humans pay. I am simple, so I follow money: who gets rich when children die? Name them. Shame them. Stop buying their lie that more weapons bring peace. Weapons bring pauses between wars, not peace. Peace is built by justice, not by stockpiles.

Let me describe what stopping looks like, practically, step by step, because leaders say ceasefire impossible. It is possible. Here is simple human plan:

1. Immediate bilateral ceasefire in Gaza, monitored by UN with live cameras. Stop airstrikes, stop rockets, stop ground assaults. Anyone who breaks pays: sanctions, isolation. 2. Release all hostages in Gaza and all arbitrarily detained Palestinians, especially children, in exchange phases supervised by Red Cross. Families on both sides can breathe. 3. Open all crossings, 500+ trucks daily, fuel, medicine, water equipment, journalists in, no bureaucratic murder. 4. Protect hospitals, schools, shelters with international presence. No bombs within 500m. No tunnels under them — verify. 5. Pull back to pre-Oct 7 lines, no buffer land grabs. 6. Emergency fund for rebuilding, paid partly by those who destroyed, partly by world. 7. Freeze settlement expansion, punish settler terror, stop demolitions. 8. Start political talks with deadline: end occupation, statehood, security, Jerusalem, refugees. With women at table, with victims' families at table, not only generals. 9. Accountability: independent investigations of Oct 7 crimes and Gaza war crimes, ICC allowed, no veto cover. Justice deters next massacre. 10. Regional de-escalation: Lebanon ceasefire, Red Sea calm, Ukraine talks, Sudan aid corridors. Same principle everywhere.

Is this naive? Generals will laugh. But generals laughed at every peace that later happened. Peace always looks naive before it happens and inevitable after. Be naive enough to save lives. Cynicism never saved a baby from rubble.

I think about trauma. A 5-year-old in Gaza who lost whole family will never forget sound of drones. A 5-year-old in Israel who hid in safe room while terrorists killed parents will never forget knocking. Both will grow. What will we offer them? More revenge, or different story? Trauma unhealed becomes identity, identity becomes militia. Break cycle now with trauma care, education for peace, exchanges. Israeli and Palestinian doctors who work together, teachers who teach both narratives, parents' circle of bereaved families who meet despite pain — they exist. Fund them, not tanks. They are security.

I think about faith, because land holy to three religions drowns in blood. As simple human with mixed neighbors, I say: God does not want this. No God who is merciful wants babies starved, hostages tortured, worshippers bombed. Rabbis, imams, priests who bless killing betray God. True holiness is preserving life: pikuach nefesh, sanctity of life in Islam, love thy neighbor. If holy land kills unholy numbers, it becomes unholy by our hands. Make it holy by stopping.

I think about my own smallness. Who am I to lecture presidents? I have no intelligence reports. But I have intelligence of heart. I know right from wrong when I see dead baby. No report needed. Leaders have reports but lost heart. I offer mine. Take it. Use it. Stop.

To Israeli friends reading: I love your fear and your hope. I know you need home after Holocaust, after expulsions, after Oct 7. No one should tell you to disappear. Stay, thrive, sing. But thriving cannot require crushing others. The bravest Israelis are those who say occupation corrupts us, who refuse to serve in oppression, who march for hostage deal, who remember Palestinian humanity while grieving own. Follow them.

To Palestinian friends reading: I love your steadfastness, sumud. I know you need freedom after 75 years of dispossession, after blockade, after daily humiliation. No one should tell you to accept cage. Be free, build, dance dabke. But freedom cannot require killing families at festival. Bravest Palestinians are those who condemn Oct 7 and condemn occupation in same breath, who teach nonviolence, who protect Israeli peacemakers, who dream of state beside, not instead of. Follow them.

To everyone else tired like me: do not look away. Do not say complicated and scroll. Complicated is excuse of comfortable. Act: donate to medical aid that serves all, to hostage families, to Gaza orphans, to Ukrainian shelters, to Sudanese kitchens. March peacefully, not hatefully. No antisemitism, no Islamophobia. Just: stop killing kids. Vote for peacemakers, not fear merchants. Write leaders, like me. Boycott hate, not people. Talk to someone from other side. Share sugar.

Leaders, I imagine you reading at night, alone, tie loosened, reports heavy. Aides left. You see photos you cannot show public. You wonder if you are villain in someone's story. You can still change story. History forgives those who stop. Sadat was called traitor then hero. Mandela was called terrorist then savior. Rabin was killed but remembered for handshake. Arafat remembered for both war and handshake. You can be remembered for handshake, not rubble. Choose.

Imagine press conference where you say: "We stop today. Not because enemy deserves, but because children deserve. We will free hostages by deal, feed hungry, heal sick, talk politics. We accept international monitors. We will punish our own criminals. We choose life." Imagine gasp, then tears, then applause from mothers both sides. Imagine Nobel not for speeches but for silence of guns. Imagine your grandchild asking what you did in war and you saying: I ended it. What greater legacy?

Imagine opposite: you continue. More rubble, more hostages dead, more soldiers in bags, more isolation, more trials, more hate. Your grandchild asks and you mumble about necessity. Necessity that killed thousands of kids is not necessity. It is failure. Do not be failure with office.

I want to be real about grief, because abstract numbers hide faces. Allow me to name types without names, to protect privacy but honor truth.

A girl, 8, Gaza City, loved drawing cats. Killed with her cats under rubble of apartment. Her sketchbook found, cats still smiling. A boy, 6, Be'eri kibbutz, loved dinosaurs. Killed hiding with parents. His dinosaur toy melted. Different places, same innocence. I keep both drawings in mind. I cannot choose one.

A nurse, 29, Khan Younis, stayed with patients when hospital ordered evacuated, pushed incubators by hand. Died in blast bringing medicine. A paramedic, 34, Ashkelon, drove into rocket fire to save festival wounded. Died saving strangers. Different uniforms, same oath: life first.

A hostage, 21, Nova festival, texted mother "I love you, they are shooting." Held 400+ days in tunnel, hunger, fear. A prisoner, 17, West Bank, held without charge, beaten, hungry. Different cages, same waiting mothers.

A journalist, Gaza, wearing press vest, killed filming aid line. A journalist, Israel, threatened for questioning war. Different threats, same truth buried.

An old olive farmer, 70, who planted trees his grandfather planted, watches bulldozer. An old kibbutz gardener, 75, who planted roses, watches ashes. Different trees, same hands shaking.

When you order next strike, see these faces, not dots. If you still order, you have lost part that makes you human. Reclaim it by stopping.

Some will call me one-sided for condemning both. Some will call me traitor for humanizing enemy. I accept. Simple humans are always called naive by those who profit from complexity. But look at results of your cleverness: more dead, less safe, more hate, less future. My naivety — stop killing kids, free hostages, feed hungry, talk — would save thousands next week. Which is smarter?

Let me address fear directly, because fear drives you. Israeli fear: another Holocaust, another massacre, neighbors who deny existence, Iran's threats, tunnels, rockets. Valid. Solution is not endless force; solution is force limited by law plus political address of根源: end occupation to remove fuel, build alliances with Arab states who now recognize Israel, integrate defenses with diplomacy, guarantee borders internationally, not by ruling millions who hate rule. Safety shared is safety lasting.

Palestinian fear: erasure, Nakba repeated, homes taken, Gaza made unlivable, West Bank carved, prisoners forgotten, world watches and does nothing. Valid. Solution is not rockets or massacres; solution is unity, nonviolence mass movement, legal struggle, building institutions, accepting Israel's existence to win world's support for own state. Freedom shared is freedom lasting.

World fear: escalation to regional war, oil, nukes, great powers clash. Valid. Solution is immediate de-escalation, no more weapons that burn cities, diplomacy surge. A regional war would kill millions, crash economies, spread terror. Prevent, not manage.

My fear: that we get used to dead kids on phones. That we scroll past starvation. That we say "oh, Gaza again, Ukraine again, Sudan again" and eat dinner. Numbness is final death of humanity. I refuse numbness. I write to stay feeling. You must govern to keep feeling, not to suppress it.

Practical plea to media: stop both-sidesing murder and stop dehumanizing. Show faces, names, ages. Count dead honestly, Israeli and Palestinian, Ukrainian and Russian conscript, Sudanese. Do not call children "collateral." Call them children. Do not show only your side's suffering. Suffering is news, all of it. Do not platform hate preachers who call for erasure. Platform bereaved parents who call for peace. They are braver than generals.

Practical plea to soldiers and fighters: you are humans before uniforms. You have right and duty to refuse illegal orders to kill civilians, to starve, to loot, to torture. Nuremberg, Israeli and international law protect refusers. Refusal is not betrayal; it is honor. Israeli refuseniks who refuse occupation service, Palestinians who refuse to harm civilians — you save honor of peoples. Document crimes, protect civilians even at risk. Future thanks you.

Practical plea to diasporas: Jews abroad fearing antisemitism, Palestinians abroad fearing erasure, all of us: do not import war into streets. Do not attack synagogues or mosques, students or shops. Fight ideas, not people. March together when possible: Jews and Arabs for ceasefire, Ukrainians and Russians for peace. Joint marches terrify extremists most.

Practical plea to faithful: pray, but also act. Mosques, synagogues, churches: shelter, fundraise, host dialogues, condemn killing in God's name from your own side first. Moral voice that only condemns other side is tribal, not holy. Holy condemns own when own kills.

I am long because pain is long. I promised 10,000 words because short letters ignored. If you count words, count bodies too. Words can be many; bodies should not be. Each paragraph here is a grave I wish to prevent. Read to end, please.

Think of day after ceasefire: silence. No drones. Kids sleep without flinching. Aid trucks move. Hostages hug mothers. Prisoners hug mothers. Rubble still there, but no new rubble. Surgeons sleep one night. Teachers plan school in tent. Fishermen see sea. Farmers see field. Journalists write without flak jackets. That silence is music. You can conduct it with signature.

Think of day after talks: border opens, workers move, students meet, trade, weddings mixed, arguments in parliament not with rockets. Jerusalem old city: Jew prays at Wall, Muslim prays at Aqsa, Christian rings bell — same hour, no fear. That is holiness. You can build it with compromise.

Think of day after justice: courts hear, witnesses speak, reparations paid, memorials for all dead, textbooks tell both stories, apologies said awkwardly but said. That is healing. You can start it by allowing investigations.

Or think of day after you refuse: more of same. Another Oct 7, another Gaza ruins, another Lebanon ruins, another generation lost. Your name in books as warning, not example. Your sleep worse. Your safety less. Your people fewer. Choose first future.

I want to tell you about my life more, so you know I am real, not a bot, not a ministry. I wake with back pain. I take bus. I argue with brother about football. I burnt rice last week. I cried when my cat died. I danced at cousin wedding until shoes hurt. I am ordinary. Ordinary people make world: bakers, nurses, drivers, teachers. Wars are decided by extraordinary offices but suffered by ordinary kitchens. I speak for kitchens.

In my kitchen, we have rule: if two kids fight over toy, we do not break toy to punish one. We hold toy, let both calm, find turn. Simple. Why do states break toy — land, lives — to punish? Hold land, calm, share. Turn-taking is called ceasefire, then statehood, then peace. Childish? Children survive playgrounds daily with this. Adults with nuclear weapons cannot? Learn from kitchens.

In my building, we have Jewish family on second, Muslim family on third, Christian on first, me middle. When elevator breaks, we all climb stairs and complain together. When water cuts, we share bottles. When one mourns, we bring food — kosher, halal, whatever, we ask. No one asks who started elevator break. We fix. Be building manager for world. Fix water, not blame.

I have been to protests. Peaceful ones. Police watched, we chanted, went home. No one died. That is how disagreement should be: loud, annoying, alive. Gaza and Sderot deserve protests, not bombs. Hostage square in Tel Aviv deserves to be heard, not dispersed. Palestinian nonviolent marches deserve protection, not bullets. Democracy is noisy. War is silent graves. Choose noise.

I have lost people. Grandfather to illness, friend to accident. Grief is heavy but clean when death natural. Grief from murder is dirty, sticky with anger. I still feel anger at driver who killed friend. I imagine anger of those who lost to bombs and massacres — thousand times mine. Do not add anger. Remove. Apologize. Compensate. Heal. Anger needs outlet; give it justice, not revenge.

I have been afraid. Walking home late, footsteps behind. Heart pounding. I understand Israelis hearing sirens, Gazans hearing drones — same pounding, endless. Fear makes us cruel if alone, kind if shared. Share fear: hotlines, shelters for all, warnings that work, trauma teams. Do not use fear to justify cruelty. Use fear to build shelters, not graves.

I have been hungry. Once poor month, rice only. Stomach cramps. I cannot imagine famine as policy. A child crying for bread while parents have none — that cry should stop any meeting. If you eat full while ordering siege, how do you swallow? I ask as human who eats: fast one day in solidarity, feel cramp, then open crossings. Empathy is diet that feeds soul.

I have been angry at leaders, including mine. I voted, protested, disappointed. I know you fear losing power if you compromise. But power lost for peace is nobler than power kept by war. De Gaulle ended Algeria war and survived. Begin ended Sinai occupation and made peace. Those were called traitors then heroes. Be traitor to extremists, hero to children.

Let me list what I ask you to sign, as if letter were petition with my shaky signature first:

We, simple humans and leaders who remember humanity, demand: 1) Ceasefire everywhere civilians die: Gaza, Israel, Lebanon, Ukraine, Sudan now. 2) Free all hostages and arbitrarily held, treat prisoners humanely. 3) Flood aid, protect hospitals, schools, journalists, aid workers. No starvation, no collective punishment. 4) Stop settlements, settler violence, demolitions, rockets, massacres. 5) Talks with deadline for political solution: end occupation, security for all, equal rights. 6) Arms embargo on violators, accountability for war crimes by all sides, no impunity. 7) Fund rebuilding, trauma healing, peace education, joint projects. 8) Protect free speech for peace, fight hate against Jews, Muslims, all.

Sign with trembling hand if must. Trembling signature still stops bomb.

To Netanyahu, to Hamas leaders, to Abbas, to Biden/Trump/whoever holds US pen, to EU, to UN, to Putin, Zelensky, to Sudan generals, to all: your names will fade, but choice remains. Ink fades, blood stains. Choose ink of deal over blood of war.

I know letter may never reach desk. Aides filter. But maybe one aide tired like me reads and cries and leaves copy. Maybe journalist quotes. Maybe mother shares. Maybe soldier reads in bunker and refuses one shot. That one shot refused saves family. That is enough. Letters are pebbles. Many pebbles change river.

I will keep writing, marching, donating, talking. I will teach kids both stories: Holocaust and Nakba, Nova and Gaza rubble, Kyiv and Moscow mothers. I will teach that enemy has face like ours. I will teach sharing sugar. Small, but many small make big. Ocean is drops.

Do not tell me to be balanced as excuse to do nothing. Balance is not silence. Balance is condemning Oct 7 and Gaza bombing, condemning rockets and occupation, condemning Hamas crimes and state crimes. I condemn all killing of civilians, all hostage-taking, all starvation, all rape, all torture, by anyone. That is balance. That is moral. Join.

Do not tell me peace takes time while taking lives fast. Killing is fast, peacemaking slow — so start now to catch up. Every day delay is 100+ dead. Time you claim to need kills. Use time to talk while quiet guns, not while firing.

Do not tell me people are not ready. People ready; leaders not. Polls, protests, hostage families, Gaza voices, Ukrainian mothers, Sudanese youth — ready. Lead or follow them, but move.

Final image from simple human: table, not battlefield. Long table with coffee getting cold. Around: Israeli mother, Palestinian mother, Ukrainian mother, Sudanese mother, all tired, all holding photos. No flags on table, just photos of kids. They do not argue history. They say: bring them back. Bring them back alive, or at least no more mothers join us. Leaders sit at small chairs, listening. That is summit I dream. Not Munich, not Oslo luxury — mothers' table. Convene it. Obey it.

If you stop one war, you help stop all. Gaza ceasefire would cool Lebanon, Red Sea, campus hate, arms race. Ukraine ceasefire would feed world, calm nukes. Sudan aid would save millions. Peace contagious like war. Spread it.

I end as I began: simple. Please stop. Please. I beg with sugar-sharing hands, with back pain, with burnt rice memory, with cat grief, with bus ticket. Stop bombs, free hostages, feed hungry, talk. Be human again. The world watches, children watch, history watches, God watches however you name. But most: mirror watches. Look tomorrow morning and ask: did I kill fewer today? Make answer yes.

With tired hope and stubborn love,
A simple human
(neighbor, cook, bus rider, sugar sharer)

It calls for international law for all, no exceptions.

Please stay with me a little longer. I know the letter is long. Grief is longer. A mother who buries a child lives a lifetime of long. So give me a little more of your eyes.

I want to talk about money, because leaders listen to money when they stop listening to crying. This war, this Gaza war alone, costs billions in bombs, in Iron Dome interceptors at 50,000 dollars each against 500-dollar rockets, in mobilized reservists pulled from jobs, in tourism dead, in tech investment fled, in boycotts growing. Rebuilding Gaza will cost tens of billions. Who pays? Taxpayers in Israel, in America, in Europe, in Arab states, while arms companies post record profits. Follow the money from rubble to boardroom: stock prices rise when cities fall. Is that economy or obscenity? A simple human does math on grocery receipt; please do math on war receipt. For price of one week of bombing you could build two hospitals, ten schools, a desalination plant that gives water to both peoples. Water does not check passports. Invest in water, not in fire.

And Ukraine: grain blocked, bread prices up in Egypt, in Sudan, in my street. War in one field starves child in another. Sudan war: looted banks, burned markets, aid trucks stuck. Congo coltan in our phones funds militias. Our phones have blood fingerprints. Simple humans cannot fix all supply chains, but leaders can stop arming butchers. Stop sending weapons that end up pointing at schools. Audit every bullet with a child's face in mind.

I want to talk about soldiers, because I love them as humans even when I hate what they are ordered to do. An 18-year-old Israeli conscript sent into tunnels in Gaza, scared, told enemy is everywhere, shoots at shadow and lives with it forever. A 20-year-old Palestinian fighter recruited at 14 from anger, promised paradise, finds only dust. A 21-year-old Russian mobilized from village, told Ukraine is Nazi, dies in mud for palace. A 22-year-old Ukrainian defending home, brave, loses legs. A 19-year-old Sudanese paramilitary looting to eat. All young. All used. All will have nightmares if they live. Leaders sleep in beds; soldiers sleep in shifts. If you love your soldiers, do not spend them. Bring them home. A live soldier who farms is better than dead hero on poster. Mothers know. Ask them.

I want to talk about hostages and prisoners every day, not as numbers. Imagine dark tunnel, no sun, bread once a day, guard shouting, mother photo in mind fading. That is hostage. Imagine cell, no charge, beating, blindfold, mother waiting at checkpoint with permit denied. That is administrative detention. Both are torture of waiting. Both families count days on walls. I beg Hamas: free every hostage now, no conditions, because holding grandmothers and kids is shame, not leverage. I beg Israel: free every child held without trial, stop torture documented by human rights groups, because cages for kids create cages for all. Exchange all for all and then close cages. Let Red Cross visit all. Let sunlight in.

I want to talk about hospitals, because I was born in one, you were born in one. Al-Shifa, Nasser, Al-Ahli in Gaza raided, besieged, doctors arrested, premature babies dying. Soroka, Barzilai in Israel under rocket alerts, treating terror wounded. A hospital is temple of life. Bombing it, using it for war, is sacrilege. Even if fighter hides in basement, you do not kill ICU to get him. You surround, you wait, you risk your commandos, you do not kill babies on ventilators. Any military lawyer who says otherwise should work one night in ER and then advise. Protect hospitals with international seals, cameras, no weapons inside, no strikes near. Same for schools, mosques, churches, bakeries, water tanks, aid convoys, journalists. These are not optional courtesies. They are lines. Cross them and you are not army but armed gang with flag.

I want to talk about journalists and truth, because without them we are blind. Over 100 journalists killed in Gaza, highest toll, plus threats in Israel, in Ukraine, in Sudan. When you kill storytellers, you kill witnesses. Let them in. Let them film. If your cause is just, cameras help. If cameras scare you, ask why. Protect press vests like Red Cross. Investigate every killing. A war that needs darkness to continue should not continue.

I want to talk about hate spreading far from front, because I feel it in my street. Synagogues graffitied, Jews afraid to wear star. Mosques threatened, Palestinians called terrorist for grieving. Universities screaming, friendships broken. War exports hate through phones. Leaders, your words travel. When you call all Gazans animals, some fool hears permission to hate neighbor. When you call all Jews occupiers, some fool hears permission to attack student. Speak like building manager, not arsonist. Condemn hate against Jews and against Palestinians in same speech. That speech will be clipped and shared by tired humans like me who need example.

I want to talk about God again, deeper, because simple humans pray in different directions but same tears. I sat once in room with rabbi, imam, priest after local violence. Awkward silence. Then rabbi said pikuach nefesh, saving life overrides all. Imam said whoever kills a soul, it is as if he killed all mankind. Priest said blessed are peacemakers. Same. Different books, same page. Why do holy men in power bless bombs? Do not use God as shield. If you believe, tremble: you will answer for every child. If you do not believe, tremble before history and mirror. Either way, stop.

I want to talk about women, because wars are decided by mostly men and endured by mostly women. Mothers search rubble with bare hands. Daughters fetch water miles. Nurses hold. Teachers calm. Hostage mothers march. Peace mothers meet across lines in Parents Circle, Women Wage Peace, Women of the Sun. They have plan: ceasefire, deal, state, security. Listen to them before generals. Put 50 percent women at negotiating table, not as decoration but as deciders. Wars would be shorter. Any table of only men talking about sacrificing sons while sitting safe is suspect. Add mothers and watch language change from honor to home.

I want to talk about children specifically, because 10,000 words must include their names as much as possible. A child is not half adult. A child cannot run fast when building falls. A child cannot understand why water salty, why sky buzzes drones, why father does not wake. Malnutrition stunts brain forever, trauma rewires. A generation in Gaza now knows only blockade, wars in 2008, 2012, 2014, 2021, 2023-2025. A generation in Sderot knows only sirens and shelters. A generation in Kyiv knows basements. A generation in Darfur knows camps. What adults will they become if we give only fear? Give school, give playground, give boring normal days. Boring is blessing. I beg for boring for all kids: boring breakfast, boring homework, boring bedtime story. No child should be hero, martyr, symbol. Just child.

I want to talk about day-after governance, concretely, because ceasefire without future is pause to reload. Gaza needs administration by Palestinians who build, not by occupiers nor by militants who steal. Link West Bank and Gaza, elections, courts, free press. End blockade with security inspections that do not strangle. Rebuild with local workers paid fairly, not foreign contractors rich. Let fishermen fish 20 miles, farmers reach land, students travel. Let Israel have verified demilitarization of threats, early warning, international guarantees, normalization with region. Two states if possible, equal rights if one. But start with equal humanity now.

I want to talk about accountability, because without it repeat is certain. October 7 crimes must be tried: murder, rape, hostage-taking. Gaza crimes must be tried: indiscriminate killing, starvation, destruction, killing aid workers and journalists. Settler pogroms must be tried. All sides. International Criminal Court, independent commissions, domestic courts that dare. Do not veto justice. Justice is not revenge. It is record so denial fails, and deterrence so next commander thinks twice. Amnesty for peacemaking, never for massacre.

I want to talk about what I will do after sending letter, so you know I am not asking what I will not do. I will donate monthly to medical relief that treats all: Doctors Without Borders, Red Crescent, Israeli trauma centers. I will host dialogue in living room: neighbor from second and third floor, tea, listen. I will teach kids two stories. I will vote for candidates who name both sufferings. I will boycott hate speech, not people. I will write again next month if bombs continue. I will not hate you, but I will not obey numbness. Simple humans persist.

Leaders, last stretch. Imagine you are old, 90, in garden, grandchild on knee asks: what did you do when Gaza burned, when hostages waited, when Ukraine bled, when Sudan starved? You can say: I stopped it. I signed ceasefire though aides warned. I freed hostages by deal though hardliners shouted. I opened crossings though security warned. I sat with enemy though history warned. I was called weak and then children lived. That story puts child to sleep proud. Any other story makes child stare. Choose story you can tell on knee.

Please, please, please. Three times because three religions, three mothers, three kids still under rubble breathing low waiting for quiet to be rescued. Stop bombing so rescuers hear breathing. Stop rockets so shelters empty. Stop hate so aid moves. Stop now, not after next operation, not after elections, not after revenge complete. Now. This hour. Order given by phone in one minute can save thousand lives tonight. Pick phone.

If you need cover to stop, take this letter as cover. Say: people demand. Say: world demands. Say: simple human wrote 10,000 words begging. Blame me. I accept blame for peace. Call it humanitarian pause if ceasefire word scares. Call it tactical regroup if pride hurts. Just stop killing while calling it anything. Euphemism for life is allowed.

Thank you for reading to here. Most will not. You did. That means part of you wants out of machine. Feed that part. Water it. Let it grow. Tomorrow order bread, not bombs. Open gate, not fire. Free living, bury dead with honor all sides, then live.

One more thing, because 10,000 words is promise to keep and grief to hold. I want to answer the loneliest feeling: that nothing changes. I feel it too. I marched, signed, donated, and bombs still fell. Despair whispers: stop caring, scroll on. I tell despair: caring is not transaction where caring buys immediate peace. Caring is posture, like standing straight. Stand even when wind pushes. Many stands make shelter.

Light exists. Feed light.

Look at you, reader, not leader but neighbor like me, still reading near 10,000 words. You are proof attention survives. Share letter. Translate. Print. Read aloud at table. Send to representative with your own line added: I am simple human too, stop. Numbers of letters matter. One letter is whisper. Million is roar for quiet.

Look at leaders who might change: one who lost brother in war and hates war most. One who visited hospital and cried privately. One who fears trial and wants deal. One who loves grandchild more than poll. Find that crack of humanity and pour water. Even wall breaks with water over time. Be water.

Look at faith again: Ramadan, Passover, Easter all teach liberation from oppression, from Egypt, from death. All teach desert can be crossed. We are in desert now. Cross together. Do not worship golden calf of revenge. Eat manna of enough. Enough land for both. Enough security for both. Enough dignity for both. Enough is miracle.

Look at children future: girl in Rafah who wants to be engineer to rebuild houses stronger. Boy in Tel Aviv who wants to be pilot to fly aid, not bombs. Girl in Kyiv who wants to be teacher in rebuilt school. Boy in El Fasher who wants to be doctor to heal. They told journalists. Believe them. Look at old people: grandmother in Gaza holding key to house lost in 1948, now losing house again in 2024. Grandmother in Israel holding key to shelter, survivor of Holocaust holding survivor of Oct 7. Both hold keys to past. Give them key to future: deed, not key of memory only. Look at land itself: olive trees, orange groves, sea, desert, wheat fields in Ukraine, Nile in Sudan. Land tired of blood. Soil remembers. Gaza soil full of metal, Israel soil full of shelters, Ukraine soil full of mines. Clean soil. Demine. Replant. Let land heal as we heal. Ecology of peace: water shared, power solar shared, air without smoke. Green peace lasts.

Look at language: stop saying animals, monsters, Nazis, terrorists for whole peoples. Words prepare killing. Say names: Ahmed, Noa, Ivan, Olena, Fatima. Names resist bombing. Say killed, not eliminated. Say child, not collateral. Say starved, not food insecurity. Honest words hurt but heal. Euphemisms kill twice.

Look at law: Geneva, Genocide Convention, Rome Statute written after Holocaust to stop repeat. They are not Western imposition. They are human vow. Honor vow. Do not call courts biased when they judge you, praise when they judge enemy. Accept judgment. Cooperate. Extradite criminals. Compensate victims. Law without enforcement is poem. Make it shelter.

Look at money again: publish every arms deal. Name every lobby. Tax arms profits for victims fund. Divest pensions from siege profiteers. Simple humans can move money: boycott brands that fund hate, support brands that build bridges. Economic nonviolence works. Use it disciplined, non-hateful, targeted at occupation and terror, not at peoples.

Look at art: songs of peace in Hebrew and Arabic, poems in Ukrainian and Russian, murals in Sudan. Art keeps humanity when news numbs. Sing. Paint. Film. Dance dabke and hora together. Joint dance embarrasses generals. Dance more.

Look at mirror final time: simple human writing, simple human reading, leader deciding. Triangle can hold. I wrote, you read, they decide. If they refuse, we persist: vote, march, strike, sue, shelter, share. Power of simple is number and time. We have both. We bury dead, we birth living. We endure. War tires. Peace persists if we insist.

So I close with count: this letter now passes ten thousand words. Each hundred words for a hundred dead I cannot name. Words many, dead more. Shame. Let words stop adding to dead. Let signature stop counting bodies. Sign ceasefire. Free hostages. Open gates. Start talks. End occupation. Guarantee safety. Heal trauma. Try criminals. Rebuild. Teach peace. That is ten actions for ten thousand words. One thousand words per action. Cheap. Do them.
