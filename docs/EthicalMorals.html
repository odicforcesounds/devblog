LEVEL 1 · CLEARANCE: PUBLIC

1. Foundations: What Are We Actually Talking About?

Promotional thesis: every peaceful, useful society runs on the same hidden machinery — shared expectations that let strangers cooperate without fear. This manual reverse-engineers that machinery so you can repair it and build more of it.

⚙ SPEC 1.1 — CORE DEFINITIONS
  • Morality (Latin mores = customs): the lived, often unwritten rules a group actually follows — what feels right/wrong, praise/blame-worthy. Example: queuing, not cheating on a partner, caring for elders.
  • Ethics (Greek ethos = character/habit): the reflective, systematic study and design of those rules. Ethics asks: are our morals justified? Can we do better?
  • Law: the enforceable minimum. Not everything immoral is illegal (lying to a friend), not everything illegal is immoral (jaywalking on an empty road).
  • Politics: ethics at population scale — who gets what, who decides, and what happens when we disagree.

1.3 Metaethics in 5 minutes

🧲
Moral realism

Moral truths exist independently of opinion (Plato, Kant, Parfit). Torturing children for fun is wrong even if a culture applauds it.

📣
Expressivism / Emotivism

Moral sentences express attitudes, not facts (Hume, Ayer, Stevenson). “Murder is wrong” ≈ “Boo murder!” + “Do not do it.”

🏗
Constructivism

Morality is what rational agents would agree to under fair conditions (Rawls, Korsgaard). Built, not found.

Related: cognitivism vs non-cognitivism (can moral claims be true/false?), relativism vs universalism (one morality or many?), error theory (Mackie: all moral claims are false because they presuppose spooky properties).

⚠ COMMON MISREADRelativism (“it’s true for them”) is a description of disagreement, not a solution to it. Engineers still need a decision procedure when cultures collide — see Level 4.

LEVEL 2 · CLEARANCE: OPERATOR

2. Normative Engines — Five Ways to Decide Right

Think of normative theories as engines: same fuel (a dilemma), different combustion. A good engineer knows all five and when each overheats.

📏
1. Deontology — Follow the rule

Kant (1785): act only on maxims you could will as universal law. Never treat persons as mere means. Duties (do not lie, do not kill) bind even if breaking them would help. Categorical Imperative Rights

Strength: protects minorities. Fails when: rules collide (lie to save a life?).

⚖
2. Consequentialism — Weigh the outcome

Bentham / Mill / Singer: right = best consequences. Classical utilitarianism: maximise total happiness (or preferences). Used in cost-benefit, triage, effective altruism. Utility QALY

Strength: impartial arithmetic. Fails when: it sacrifices one innocent for the many.

🌳
3. Virtue ethics — Be the person

Aristotle: cultivate excellences — courage, temperance, justice, wisdom — until good action flows from character. Ask “What would the virtuous person do?” Eudaimonia Habit

Strength: handles everyday life. Fails when: you need a split-second rule.

🤲
4. Care & Relational ethics

Gilligan, Noddings: morality grows from concrete relationships and vulnerability, not abstract rules. Attend, respond, repair. Central to nursing, teaching, parenting. Responsibility

⚖️
5. Justice as fairness

Rawls (1971): design society from behind a veil of ignorance — not knowing if you’ll be rich/poor, man/woman, majority/minority. You’d pick equal liberties + inequalities only if they help the worst-off (difference principle). Try the simulator below.

🕊
Hybrid: principlism

Real hospitals use four mid-level principles: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice (Beauchamp & Childress). No single engine — a dashboard. This is how production ethics actually works.

3.2 Haidt's Moral Foundations (why left & right feel different)

Psychologist Jonathan Haidt shows liberals weight care + fairness most; conservatives weight those plus loyalty, authority, sanctity. Same facts, different taste buds. If you want to persuade across tribes, speak to their foundations — crucial for Level 5.

LEVEL 4 · CLEARANCE: BUILDER

4. Production Manual — How to Produce Ethics (Not Just Admire It)

Ethics is manufactured like anything reliable: design → test → deploy → monitor → repair. Here is the assembly line for a person, a team, a company, and an AI.

4.1 Personal rig (daily maintenance)

  1. Values inventory (30 min, once): list 5 non-negotiables + 5 aspirations. Example: honesty, care for parents, craft, fairness, ecological restraint.
  2. Pre-commitments: “If offer involves lying to a customer, I decline automatically.” Removes willpower from the hot moment.
  3. Evening review (3 min): What did I do today that my ideal self would endorse? One repair tomorrow.
  4. Friction design: make good easy (fruit visible, donations automatic), bad hard (delete gambling app, 24h rule for angry emails).

4.2 Team / company OS (copy-paste template)

4.3 AI & product checklist (applied ethics in code)

✓ PRE-SHIP CHECKLIST — FORM ME-04
  1. Who is harmed if we are wrong? Can they appeal to a human in <48h?
  2. What does the dataset over/under-represent? Test worst-off group first.
  3. Can the user say no, understand why, and leave with their data?
  4. What does misuse at scale look like? Rate-limit, watermark, audit trail?
  5. Who signed off? Name + date. No anonymous launches.

5.2 Country dossiers — locate the real parties

🇺🇸 USA — Democrats vs Republicans

Democrats (centre-left liberal): Obamacare expansion, climate funds (Inflation Reduction Act 2022), abortion rights. Republicans (conservative, increasingly populist under Trump): tax cuts (TCJA 2017), gun rights, immigration restriction, 2024 platform of tariffs + deportations. Ethical split: care/fairness vs loyalty/authority/liberty (Haidt). De-quality to watch both sides: legislative gridlock + gerrymandering + money in politics (Citizens United 2010).

🇬🇧 UK — Labour vs Conservatives (+ LibDems, Reform, Greens, SNP)

Labour (Starmer, 2024 landslide): NHS rescue, workers’ rights, green prosperity plan (trimmed). Tories (2010–2024): austerity, Brexit (2016/2020), “levelling up” (under-delivered). Lesson: Brexit as consequentialism failure — promised £350m/week for NHS, delivered trade friction (OBR: −4% long-run productivity).

🇩🇪 Germany — CDU/CSU · SPD · Greens · FDP · AfD · Linke

Proportional system forces coalitions (“traffic light” SPD+Greens+FDP 2021–2024 collapsed over debt brake). AfD (far right, monitored by domestic intelligence in several states) shows de-quality: protest channeled into anti-immigrant framing. Quality: federalism + Constitutional Court as ethics hardware.

🇫🇷 France — Renaissance (centre) · RN (far right) · LFI (radical left) · LR · Greens

Two-round system + 2024 snap election → hung parliament. RN (Le Pen/Bardella): immigration + purchasing power. LFI (Mélenchon): retirement at 60, disobey EU budget rules. Macron’s pension reform (49.3, 2023, age 62→64) = case study in procedure vs legitimacy.

🇮🇳 India — BJP vs Congress (+ AAP, regional)

BJP (Modi, Hindu-nationalist, developmentalist): growth + welfare delivery (toilets, UPI) paired with majoritarian de-quality (press-freedom decline, RSF rank ~159/180 in 2024; 2019 citizenship law protests). Congress (secular, dynastic): rights + redistribution but organisational decay. Lesson: delivery without pluralism is brittle.

🇧🇷 Brazil — PT (left) vs PL (Bolsonarist right)

PT (Lula): Bolsa Família lifted ~20m from extreme poverty (2003–2014) — quality; Mensalão/Petrobras scandals — de-quality (corruption). Bolsonaro/PL: anti-corruption + evangelical + agribusiness; Amazon deforestation +27% 2019–2021; Jan 8 2023 coup attempt = democratic de-quality textbook case.

🇿🇦 South Africa — ANC · DA · EFF · MK

ANC (liberation → dominance → 2024 loss of majority, now unity government): Mandela quality vs Zuma-era “state capture” de-quality (Zondo Commission). DA (liberal, good municipal audit) vs EFF (red berets, expropriation without compensation). Lesson: without alternation, ethics debt compounds.

🇯🇵 Japan — LDP (dominant conservative) vs CDP/JCP/Ishin

LDP (almost continuous rule since 1955): stability + bureaucracy quality; faction slush-fund scandal 2023–24 de-quality. Shows conservative failure mode: longevity → opacity. Opposition fragmented = accountability gap.

⚠ HOW TO READ ANY MANIFESTO IN 10 MINUTES (FIELD PROCEDURE)
  1. Who pays, who benefits, who decides? (follow the money + power)
  2. What is the theory of change — rules, incentives, or character? (Level 2 engines)
  3. What happens to the worst-off + the unborn + the outsider? (veil test)
  4. What check limits them when they are wrong? (courts, press, alternation)
  5. What did they do last time they had power? (revealed preference > promise)

LEVEL 6 · CLEARANCE: ARCHITECT

6. Blueprint for Peaceful, Useful Societies + Catalogue of Bad Decisions

6.1 Seven new (but buildable) ideas

  1. Ethics maintenance week: like fire drills — annual school/workplace week where everyone runs a trolley, a commons game, and a budget veil exercise. Peace is a skill, not a mood.
  2. Citizens’ assemblies by lot: 100 random citizens, paid, briefed by experts, decide one thorny issue (Ireland did this for abortion 2018; France for climate 2020). Beats rage-algorithms.
  3. Commons trusts: atmosphere, aquifers, attention — managed by Ostrom-style trusts with caps + dividends (Alaska Permanent Fund pays oil dividends; extend to carbon).
  4. Democracy hardware: independent courts, public media funding, proportional top-up seats, anti-gerrymandering commissions, 18-year-old candidacy.
  5. Usefulness score for policy: every law ships with QALYs + carbon + worst-off impact on page 1 — like nutrition labels.
  6. Repair funds: truth-and-reconciliation + compensation (South Africa TRC, Canada settlements, German Wiedergutmachung) — peace without repair is amnesia.
  7. AI mediators, human deciders: use models to summarise, translate, find win-wins (e.g., Polis deliberation platform used in Taiwan vTaiwan), but final vote stays human and public.
