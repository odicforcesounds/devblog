道 · The Way · I

The Way That Does Not Say Its Name

after the first chapter, in a smaller voice

You will not find it in the sentence.
The sentence is a hand.
The way is not the thing the hand holds.

Say it and you have made a fence.
Keep silent and the fence is yours to walk through,
whistling, with your hands in your sleeves.

It was not born. It borrows birth.
It does not rule. It lets the ten thousand things
argue themselves into a world.

A road is the least of it — a road is what remains
after walking. The way is what was never
a road at all, and is still under your feet.

道

道 · The Way · II

The Map Has No Author

on the hand that copies and never goes

The map has no author.
Someone walked, and the walking made a line,
and the line was copied by a hand
that never left the house.

Fold it and the creases are the only hills.
Open it and the weather is wrong.

A map with no author is still a map.
A way with no author is still the way.

道

道 · The Way · III

Two Roads and Neither

at the fork, the sign was cut elsewhere

At the fork, a sign points left and right.
Both signs were cut by the same hand,
and the hand was never at the fork.

Choose, and the unchosen road
keeps walking without you.

There is no third road.
There is only the ground you stood on
while deciding.

道

道 · The Way · IV

The Name on the Gate

a handle is not a welcome

A gate with a name above it
invites you to become the name.

Walk through without reading
and the garden is larger by one gate.

The name was a handle for strangers.
You were never a stranger to the garden.

道

道 · The Way · V

A Hand Opens; a Hand Closes

on being poor in the useful way

A hand opens: the world arrives.
A hand closes: the world is yours.

Open longer than you close
and you will be poor
in the way that leaves room.

Nobody will thank you for the room.
They will stand in it.

道

道 · The Way · VI

The Road That Forgets You

not cruelty, just maintenance

The road forgets each traveller
an inch behind the heel.

That is not cruelty.
It is how the road stays a road
instead of a museum of footsteps.

Take the forgetting as a kindness.
It lets you come back as someone else.

道

道 · The Way · VII

What the River Knows First

the shape is decided before the question

The river learns the shape of the valley
before the valley knows it has a shape.

Nothing has been decided.
The water is already there.

When the rain finally asks,
the answer has been waiting in the low ground
for a thousand years.

道

道 · The Way · VIII

The Way Is Not Ahead

only the dust behind the step

The way is not ahead of you,
waiting to be found.

It is behind you,
in the dust of the step you have already taken,
saying nothing.

Turn around and it is still behind you.
That is the joke the old men kept
from the students.

無 · The Hollow · I

Thirty Spokes

on the usefulness of what is not there

Thirty spokes, one hub.
The wheel turns on the hole you cannot see.

Clay is worked into a bowl:
it is the hollow that holds the rice.
Doors and windows are cut into a wall:
it is the emptiness you live in.

So the useful is what we shape,
and the useful-useful is what we left out.

Build a house out of everything you own
and you will sleep in the gaps,
if you sleep at all.

無

無 · The Hollow · II

The Room Without Furniture

let them bring the chair

Move the chairs out.
Now the room can be entered
without an apology.

Wait a day.
Someone will bring a chair.
Let them. Sit on the floor.

The floor is not a protest.
It is simply where the room is.

無

無 · The Hollow · III

A Cup, a Door, a Window

three teachers with no opinions

A cup is useful because it is empty.
A door is useful because it opens.
A window is useful because it is not a wall.

You are useful for the same reason,
on the days you do not insist on being full.

Fill the cup and it is only a cup with water in it.
Nothing is wrong with that.
But the tea is later.

無

無 · The Hollow · IV

The Hole in the Argument

the quiet step through

Every argument has a hole.
The clever hold it up to the light
and call it evidence.

The quiet step through it.

Outside, the argument is still going,
smaller now, and very well lit.

無 · The Hollow · V

What the Wall Is For

the answer is the sky

The wall is not the house.
The wall is the question the house was asked.

Cut a window in it.
The answer is the sky.

Some questions are answered by removing a piece of them
and letting the weather in.

無

無 · The Hollow · VI

The Silence Between Two Names

the room the thing can stand in

Say a name, and the silence after it
is the only room the thing can stand in.

Give it a second name and the room is crowded.
Give it three and the thing leaves.

Leave the first one alone.
It is doing more than you think.

無

無 · The Hollow · VII

An Unpainted Corner

the painter was wise, or tired

An unpainted corner of the ceiling:
the painter was wise, or tired.

Either way, the corner has kept
a little of the original night.

Look up on a bad day.
It is still there, and it still does not need you.

無

無 · The Hollow · VIII

The Weight of Nothing

what is missing has a body

Nothing weighs nothing until it is missing.
Then it weighs the whole afternoon,
the chair, the cup,
the sentence you were about to say.

Put the sentence down carefully.
It is heavier than it looks,
and it is not yours to carry.

水 · Water · I

Water Keeps the Low Place

nothing is softer, nothing gets further

Water has no argument.
It wins the stone by staying, not by striking.

It takes the shape of the jar and is not owned by it.
It falls the length of the mountain
and arrives without a lesson.

Men climb to be seen.
Water goes down and is everywhere.

Be the low place.
The valley does not chase the river;
the river cannot pass it by.

水

水 · Water · II

The Stone Learns Patience

the same lesson, a million times

The stone is a slow student.
Water teaches the same lesson
a million times
without raising its voice.

The stone graduates.

You will not be there for the ceremony.
That is the tuition.

水

水 · Water · III

Rain Does Not Aim

accused of missing nothing

Rain does not aim.
It arrives everywhere at once
and is accused of missing nothing.

Take the umbrella.
The rain will not be offended.

It has no list of who was grateful.

水

水 · Water · IV

The Sea Refuses Nothing

it returns what it is given as weather

The sea refuses nothing
and belongs to no one.

Bring it your worst.
It will return it as weather.

Stand on the shore long enough
and you will learn the difference
between an apology and a tide.

水

水 · Water · V

A Cup of River

the thirst was not asking for the whole river

A cup of river
is not the river.
Drink it anyway.

The thirst was never asking
for the whole river.

The river will not mind.
It has been passing this way
since before there were cups.

水

水 · Water · VI

The Soft Thing Wins

ask the tongue, ask the teeth

The soft thing wins
not by being soft
but by not having a shape
to break.

Ask the tongue.
Ask the teeth, if any are left.

In the end the mouth is a small argument
that water settles quietly.

水

水 · Water · VII

Water in a Broken Jar

no complaint about the jar

Water in a broken jar
does not complain about the jar.

It finds the floor,
the root, the seed,
and is a river by morning.

The jar, for its part,
has never felt so useful.

水

水 · Water · VIII

The Current Under the Ice

do not mistake the surface for the argument

The current under the ice
is still a current.

Do not mistake the surface
for the argument.

Winter is a long sentence
with the verb held back.
It ends the way rivers end:
all at once, downhill.

水

谷 · The Valley · I

The Valley Answers

on keeping a room no one enters

Shout at a mountain and it gives your voice back.
Shout into a valley and it gives you your voice back
with the sound taken out.

The valley is not generous.
The valley is empty, and empty is a kind of answer.

Keep a room in yourself that no one enters.
Not a secret — a space.

A guest who arrives at midnight
should find the lantern already low,
the door unlatched, and no one waiting to be thanked.

谷

谷 · The Valley · II

The Echo Has No Owner

the shout is yours

The shout is yours.
The echo is the mountain's answer,
and it belongs to whoever is standing there.

Stand there. Answer nothing.

It is the only reply the mountain
has ever accepted.

谷

谷 · The Valley · III

The Hollow Between Hills

nobody chose it for the view

The hollow between hills
is where the village is built.

Nobody chose it for the view.
They chose it because the wind goes around,
and the water comes down,
and neither has to be asked twice.

谷

谷 · The Valley · IV

A Room With One Lantern

two lanterns make an argument about shadows

A room with one lantern
is not a dark room.

A room with two lanterns
is an argument about shadows.

Choose the lantern.
Then turn it down
until the corners come back.

谷

谷 · The Valley · V

What the Mountain Sends Down

water, stone, a rumour

The mountain sends down
what it cannot keep:
water, stone, a rumour.

Be the valley.
Take it, and let it pass.

The mountain does not come to check
what you did with the rumour.

谷

谷 · The Valley · VI

The Guest at Midnight

the lantern already low

A guest at midnight
should find the lantern low,
the door unlatched,
and no one waiting to be thanked.

Most hospitality is a performance.
This is the other kind:
a house that does not need to watch you eat.

谷 · The Valley · VII

The Empty Chair at the Table

the absent behave better than we do

The empty chair at the table
is not a failure of company.

It is where the absent sit,
and they are better behaved than we are.

They never interrupt.
They never ask for the salt.
They are the reason the meal is quiet enough
to taste.

谷 · The Valley · VIII

Low Ground, Long Memory

a line of stones that used to be a bank

Low ground keeps the memory of water
long after the water has gone:
a darker green, a bent reed,
a line of stones that used to be a bank.

The land remembers what the map forgot,
and the map is the one
people trust in a drought.

樸 · The Uncarved · I

Uncarved

the block before the chisel

Before the chisel, the block.
Before the block, the tree.
Before the tree, a seed that knew nothing of tables.

The uncarved is not a failure of the carving.
It is what the carving was for,
and what it spends.

Sharpen your name and you will need it.
Put it down and the wood is still warm.

Let the grain decide the shape.
The chisel was never the author.

樸

樸 · The Uncarved · II

The Grain Decides

the plane is only a witness

The grain decides,
and the plane is only a witness.

A carpenter who argues with the grain
builds a beautiful thing
that cracks in March.

Listen to the wood.
It has been waiting longer than you.

樸

樸 · The Uncarved · III

A Block With No Name

a very expensive block

A block with no name
can be a table, a boat, a door, a bowl.

Give it a name too early
and you have made a very expensive block.

Names are chisels.
Use them when the shape is already there.

樸

樸 · The Uncarved · IV

Before the First Cut

measure twice, then put the ruler down

Before the first cut, the whole.

After the first cut, the whole
and a pile of what the whole was not.

Measure twice.
Then put the ruler down,
and look at the wood again.

樸

樸 · The Uncarved · V

The Carpenter Puts Down the Chisel

leave one joint loose

The carpenter puts down the chisel
and the room is finished.

A finished room is an argument
that has stopped moving.

Leave one joint loose.
Let the house breathe
and the next person adjust something.

樸 · The Uncarved · VI

A Knot the Plane Refuses

where the tree remembered something

A knot the plane refuses
is not an error in the wood.
It is where the tree remembered something.

Work around it.
The table will be honest,
and the guests will put their glasses
exactly there.

樸

樸 · The Uncarved · VII

The Table Remembers the Tree

the rings under the varnish

The table remembers the tree
in the rings under the varnish,
in the place where the branch was.

Guests put their glasses there
without knowing why.

That is how the past is served:
not as a story,
as a slightly uneven surface.

樸

樸 · The Uncarved · VIII

Sandpaper and Patience

choose the one that does not speak

Sandpaper and patience
make the same shape slowly.

One of them will outlast you.
Choose the one that does not speak.

The roughness goes.
The board stays a board.
You stay the one who waited.

反 · Return · I

Return Is the Movement

the moon does not fall upward

What goes out is a breath.
What comes back is also a breath,
and you did not do either.

The moon does not fall upward.
It returns, which is the only way it rises.

Fold the map when the road is clear.
The end of the argument is a door,
and the door is where you came in.

Do not be ashamed of going back.
The tide is not embarrassed by the shore.

反

反 · Return · II

The Tide Is Not Ashamed

the road has been holding your place

The tide is not ashamed
of the shore it keeps returning to.

Go back.
The road has been holding your place.

It will not ask where you have been.
It is a road, not a mother.

反

反 · Return · III

The Arrow Comes Back as a Question

the archer comes back as a hand

The arrow comes back as a question.
The archer comes back as a hand.

Aim at the target
and you will need a target forever.

The best shot is the one
after which no one can say
who was shooting.

反

反 · Return · IV

The Map in the Pocket

a square of paper with opinions

The map, folded small,
is a square of paper with opinions.

Carry it, but walk by the ground.
The paper will forgive you.

Every pocket is a small country
where the way rests between walks.

反

反 · Return · V

The Door You Came In

the threshold is where you live

Every house has a door
you came in by and forgot.

Stand there a moment.
The room looks different from the threshold,
and the threshold is where you live.

Not inside. Not outside.
In the small weather between.

反

反 · Return · VI

The Moon Does Not Fall Upward

a circle is what a return looks like from far away

The moon does not fall upward.
It returns, which is the only way it rises.

Do not call it a circle.
A circle is what a return looks like
from far away.

Stand closer.
Nothing is repeating.
Everything is coming back.

反

反 · Return · VII

Autumn Does Not Apologise

where the roots can read them

Autumn does not apologise
for taking the leaves back.

It puts them on the ground,
where the roots can read them.

You will call it loss for a week.
The tree calls it arithmetic.

反

反 · Return · VIII

The Wheel's Fourth Turn

the whole of progress, the whole of return

The wheel's fourth turn
is the first turn again,
except that the cart has moved
and the road is a little older.

That is the whole of progress.
That is the whole of return.

Do not ask which one you are doing.
The road already knows.

德 · Virtue · I

Virtue Does Not Carry a Lamp

on doing the small thing and forgetting it

A good act that asks for witnesses
is a lamp carried at noon.

The river does not announce that it is wet.
The tree does not keep a record of its shade.

Do the small thing and forget the doing.
The forgetting is not modesty.
It is what keeps the hand light.

A full hand cannot take anything.
An empty one is always being offered something.

德

德 · Virtue · II

The Hand That Forgets

light enough to do another

The hand that forgets the good it did
stays light enough to do another.

The hand that keeps a list
is heavy by the third line.

Lists are for groceries.
They are terrible at gratitude,
and worse at hands.

德

德 · Virtue · III

A Favour Without a Ledger

a strange rate of interest

A favour without a ledger
is a gift.

A favour with a ledger
is a loan at a very strange interest.

The borrower always forgets.
The ledger never does.
That is the whole of the trouble.

德

德 · Virtue · IV

The Bridge Does Not Ask

neither of them keeps a receipt

The bridge does not ask
who is crossing or why.

It holds.
The river beneath does the same,
and neither of them keeps a receipt.

Cross.
The bridge has already forgotten your name,
and that is why it can hold you.

德

德 · Virtue · V

Bread and No Applause

already doing its work in another room

Bake the bread.
Do not stand at the window
waiting for the smell to be praised.

The bread is already doing its work
in another room.

Hunger will find it.
Hunger is the only honest critic.

德

德 · Virtue · VI

The Shadow of a Good Deed

step out of the light

The shadow of a good deed
is the good deed asking for recognition.

Step out of the light
and the shadow has nowhere to fall.

The deed stays.
It was never the shadow's to keep.

德 · Virtue · VII

What the Well Gives

it has never seen the sky

The well gives water
to the rope, the bucket, the stranger.

It does not ask what the water is for.
It has never seen the sky.

That is not ignorance.
It is the deepest kind of knowledge:
enough to keep giving.

德

德 · Virtue · VIII

The Gift That Leaves No Receipt

which is the point

A gift that leaves no receipt
cannot be returned
and cannot be invoiced.

The receiver will remember it
longer than the giver,
which is the point.

Give like that once.
You will not need to practise.

猴 · The Monkey · I

The Monkey Counts to One

1 + 1 = 1, or 3 — the small hands and the unbroken count

One and one is one, or three —
the Monkey holds the abacus and the peach,
and both are the same afternoon.

Tiger came from the west with a white coat and a question.
Dragon came from the east with a mouth full of fire and a law.
The Monkey sat between them and counted the space.

Wukong carried a mountain and called it a hat.
The mountain did not mind.
Carry what is given; do not call it weight.

Nineteen-eighty the metal Monkey came in peace.
Twenty-sixteen the fire Monkey lit the fuse.
The same small hands. The same unbroken count.

猴

猴 · The Monkey · II

Tiger at the Western Gate

the question is not the one on the sign

Tiger at the western gate
wears white and asks one question,
and the question is not the one on the sign.

Answer with your eyes.
Tiger can hear a lie at forty paces,
and forgives one at fifty.

Do not bring a better lie.
Bring the truth, badly told.
Tiger prefers the effort.

猴

猴 · The Monkey · III

Dragon With a Mouth of Fire

courage is what Dragon eats for lunch

Dragon with a mouth of fire
guards the eastern road and the law.

Bring water, not courage.
Courage is what Dragon eats for lunch.

The water does not have to be much.
A cup will do.
Dragon respects the gesture.

猴

猴 · The Monkey · IV

The Space Between the Two

exactly the width of a Monkey

The space between Tiger and Dragon
is exactly the width of a Monkey.

Do not widen it.
Do not stand in it.
Count, and it holds.

When they turn to look at each other,
look at the space.
That is where the counting lives.

猴 · The Monkey · V

Wukong's Hat

something about hats

Wukong's hat was a mountain,
which tells you something about hats.

Wear what you are given.
Do not ask the mountain to be lighter,
ask your neck to be honest.

The mountain will not move.
It is not that kind of mountain.

猴

猴 · The Monkey · VI

The Peach and the Abacus

neither was the point

The peach and the abacus
were on the same table.

One was counted, one was eaten.
Neither was the point.

The point was the table,
and the afternoon,
and the small hands that could reach both.

猴 · The Monkey · VII

Small Hands, Long Memory

the space is still there in the morning

Small hands, long memory:
the Monkey remembers the count
after the numbers have gone home.

One, and one, and the space.
The space is still there in the morning.

That is what the Monkey keeps:
not the sum,
the room where the sum was made.

玄 · The Dark · I

The Dark Is Not Empty

on the part that kept its name

The dark is not the absence of the light.
It is the room the light was given.

Look at the space between two stars
and you have found the larger body.

Do not fill the silence to be rid of it.
The silence is not waiting for your sentence.

What you cannot say
is not a failure of your mouth.
It is the part of the way
that kept its name.

玄 · The Dark · II

Between Two Stars

you have been looking at the sparks

Between two stars
is the larger body.

You have been looking at the sparks
and calling it the sky.

Nothing is wrong with sparks.
But the sky is where you live,
and the sky is mostly between.

玄

玄 · The Dark · III

The Silence That Kept Its Name

the room where someone has just stopped speaking

The silence that kept its name
is not the silence of an empty room.
It is the silence of a room
where someone has just stopped speaking.

Stay.
It will say it.

The room is not waiting for you.
It is waiting with you,
which is different.

玄

玄 · The Dark · IV

The Unsaid Sentence

the best guest you have

The unsaid sentence
sits at the table all evening,
eating nothing,
and is the best guest you have.

It will leave when the plates are cleared.
It will not ask to stay the night.

Say it in the morning
and it will be a different sentence,
and the table will be colder.

玄

玄 · The Dark · V

A Door in the Wall of Words

most people knock and call the knocking language

There is a door in the wall of words.
It has no sign, and it is not locked.

Most people knock on the wall
and call the knocking language.

The door is beside the knocking.
It has always been beside it.
Put your hand out.

玄

玄 · The Dark · VI

The Last Page Is a Door

the book has not changed; you have

The last page is a door
that opens on the first page
with a little more dust on the handle.

Read it again.
The book has not changed.
You have.

That is what a door is for:
not to leave the house,
but to come back into it
from the other side.

Colophon

The poems are mine; the ideas are older than my hands. Laozi lent the hollow of the bowl and the hub of the wheel (chapter eleven), water and the low place (eight and seventy-eight), the valley (six and twenty-eight), the uncarved block, pu (fifteen, twenty-eight, thirty-seven), return as the movement of the way (forty), virtue that does not display itself (thirty-eight and fifty-one), and the name that cannot be named (one and thirty-two). Zhuangzi lent the useless tree, the butterfly and the fast of the mind.

The Monkey, the Tiger and the Dragon are not from the classics. They come from OdicforceSounds, where the Monkey is the condition of being the expression of time, the White Tiger stands in the west and the Fire Dragon in the east, and the whole argument is settled by a small equation: 1 + 1 = 1, or 3. Two of the years in the fifty-seventh poem are real — 1980, the year of the metal Monkey, and 2016, the year of the fire Monkey — and the phrase “we came in peace” is borrowed, not invented.

Nothing here is quoted. If a line sounds familiar, it is because the way has been said badly many times, and the older bad sayings are better than the new good ones.
