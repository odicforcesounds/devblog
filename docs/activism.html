CHAPTER 1 · FOUNDATIONS

What activism is (and isn't)

Definition → benefits → ethics lock. Estimated time: 15 min.

0.1 Definition, engineering-style

Activism = organised, public effort to shift power toward a defined outcome, without violence. Think of it as a system with 5 parts:

INPUT (grievance + people) → ORGANISE (meetings, roles) → SIGNAL (march, petition, strike, art) → LEVERAGE (media, money, votes, reputation) → OUTPUT (law, policy, norm, resignation)

Benefits, documented: (1) Policy wins — clean air acts, voting rights, independence; (2) Civic health — participants vote more, trust more; (3) Deterrence — officials behave better when watched; (4) Skills — public speaking, logistics, media; (5) Belonging — loneliness drops in organised groups.

0.2 Spectrum: from quiet to loud (all peaceful)

Hard rule of this manual: peaceful only. No sabotage that endangers people, no hate targeting protected groups, no disinformation. If a tactic needs secrecy because it harms, it is not in this book.

CHAPTER 2 · POWER ANALYSIS

Pillars, Overton & the 3.5% rule

Why rulers need you more than you need them.

1.1 Pillars of support (Gene Sharp)

No ruler rules alone. Power stands on pillars: police, workers, merchants, media, clergy, bureaucracy, transport. Pull pillars away (strike, boycott, resignations) and the roof wobbles. You don't push the roof — you remove its supports.

1.2 Overton window + 3.5% rule

Overton window = what the public currently calls "reasonable". Activism drags it: today's "radical" (e.g. weekend, women's vote, smoking ban) becomes tomorrow's normal. Chenoweth 3.5% rule: every movement that mobilised ~3.5% of the population in sustained nonviolent action succeeded (dataset: 323 campaigns 1900–2006). For a city of 1M, that's 35,000 active people — not a majority.

CHAPTER 3 · STRATEGY

Theory of change + Sharp's 198 methods

Stop doing random protests. Build a machine.

2.1 Theory of change (fill this before any march)

IF we mobilise [WHO: 5,000 market women + 200 drivers] DO [WHAT: boycott + sit-in at council, 30 days] THEN [DECISION-MAKER: mayor] WILL [concede: water price rollback] BECAUSE [leverage: election in 6 months + press coverage] ELSE [plan B: strike + court petition]

SMART demand test: Specific? Measurable? Achievable? Relevant? Time-bound? "Stop injustice" fails. "Rollback water tariff by 1 June" passes.

CHAPTER 4 · PRODUCTION LAB

How to produce a campaign like a studio

Message → visuals → print → video → press. With checklists.

3.1 Message formula (fill-in)

WE are [mothers of district X]. WE WANT [clean water at 2¢/litre]. THEY [council + contractor] block it for [profit]. WE WILL [march Sat 10:00 + boycott payments] UNTIL [rollback]. JOIN: [place, time, phone].

Rules: 8-word chant max. One demand per poster. Victim → villain → vision → action.

3.2 Visual identity in 1 hour (no designer)

Pick: 1 colour 1 font 1 symbol. Example: black + yellow + raised fist / water drop. Contrast ratio ≥ 4.5:1. Test poster from 10 m: readable in 3 seconds?

Try it: open any text editor, set font size 120, type demand, print A3, photograph from 10 m.

CHAPTER 5 · COUNTRY TESTING LABS

Real cases, dissected like lab experiments

Click each lab. Same template: context → tactic → result → steal-this-move.

LAB A · INDIA 1930 — Salt March (Gandhi)

Context: British salt tax + monopoly. Hypothesis: a simple, illegal-but-peaceful act (making salt) can unite peasants, merchants, Hindus, Muslims. Protocol: 388 km walk, Ahmedabad→Dandi, 24 days, 78 starters → thousands join; make salt, boycott cloth/liquor. Result: 60,000+ arrests, world press, Round Table talks; template for mass noncooperation. Steal this: pick a grievance a child can explain + a law anyone can peacefully break + a long walk (cameras love journeys).

TACTIC: march + boycottCOST: feet

LAB B · USA 1955–56 — Montgomery Bus Boycott

Context: Segregated buses; Rosa Parks arrested 1 Dec 1955. Protocol: 381-day boycott, carpools (300+ cars), mass meetings at churches, legal case Browder v. Gayle. Result: Supreme Court desegregates buses; MLK emerges; model: economic pain + legal track + church logistics. Steal this: run boycott + court case in parallel; publish carpool maps; count money lost by opponent weekly.

TACTIC: boycott + courtsDURATION: 381 days

LAB C · SERBIA 2000 — Otpor! vs Milošević

Context: Authoritarian elections. Protocol: youth brand (fist logo), humour ("Gotov je! / He's finished"), rock concerts as registration drives, poll-watchers (30,000), general strike after fraud. Result: Milošević falls 5 Oct 2000. Steal this: one logo everywhere, humour beats fear, train election observers before voting day, have a day-after plan (strike + parliament rally).

TACTIC: branding + election defenceWEAPON: humour

LAB D · SOUTH AFRICA 1955–1994 — From Freedom Charter to ballot

Context: Apartheid laws. Protocol (nonviolent strand): 1955 Freedom Charter (3,000 delegates), consumer boycotts, 1976 Soweto student marches, 1980s township stay-aways + international divestment (universities/banks pull money), 1994 election. Result: Apartheid ends; lesson: internal noncooperation × external money pressure. Steal this: write a 1-page Charter of demands; ask diaspora to target one bank/university at a time.

TACTIC: charter + divestment

LAB E · NIGERIA 2020 — #EndSARS

Context: Police brutality (SARS unit). Protocol: Twitter/X + Instagram livestreams, feminist coalition fund ledger (public spreadsheet), legal aid hotlines, food/medical tents at Lekki Toll Gate, diaspora demos in 20 cities. Result: SARS disbanded (announced), judicial panels; crackdown at Lekki 20 Oct 2020 — lesson on risk. Steal this: publish money live (trust), stream everything (deterrence), but always have exit routes + medics (see Level 5).

TACTIC: hashtag + street camp + open ledger

LAB F · GLOBAL 2018→ — Fridays for Future / Climate

Context: Climate inaction. Protocol: Greta Thunberg solo school strike → #FridaysForFuture toolkit (posters, map of strikes), 7.6M participants Sept 2019, divestment + lawsuits (Urgenda NL, Neubauer DE). Result: EU Green Deal debates, fossil divestment $40T+ pledged by institutions. Steal this: weekly rhythm (every Friday = press habit), starter kit anyone can copy, pair street + lawsuit.

TACTIC: rhythm + replication

CHAPTER 6 · FIELD OPERATIONS

Safety, law & de-escalation

The chapter that keeps you alive and out of jail. Read twice.

5.1 Safety cell (5 roles)

MARSHAL MEDIC LEGAL MEDIA SCOUT

Marshal: pace + edges. Medic: water, first aid, allergies list. Legal: lawyer number written on arm, bail plan. Media: films police, not protesters' faces without consent. Scout: 2 exits, toilet, water, shade mapped.

Go-bag: ID, cash, water, snack, power bank, scarf, meds, emergency contact. Never bring: weapons, alcohol, anything you can't afford to lose. Tell someone your return time.

5.2 Law in 5 lines (general — check your country!)

1. Right to peaceful assembly exists in most constitutions + ICCPR Art. 21 — but notice/permits may be required.
2. Police may set time/place/manner limits; blanket bans are suspect.
3. You usually may film police in public; do not obstruct.
4. Civil disobedience = knowingly breaking an unjust rule + accepting legal consequence — get a lawyer before.
5. If detained: name, silence, lawyer. Don't sign, don't unlock phone without advice.

5.3 De-escalation script (memorise)

1. HANDS visible, VOICE low: "We are peaceful. We leave at [time]." 2. DISTANCE: 2 m buffer. Never touch, never chase. 3. AGENT PROVOCATEUR? Chant leaders call: "Sit! Cameras on!" Sit down. 4. POLICE ORDER? Repeat loudly so all hear. Comply slowly. Ask: "Are we detained or free to go?" 5. AFTER: write timeline within 24h. Save video in 2 places.
