Portugal on the ground

Real examples from recent years

These are not trophies or moral counter-examples. They are cases to study: what worked, what failed, which alliances were built, and which questions remain open. The sources are at the end of the section.

01 · OCTOBER 2026

Housing and the cost of living

On 17 October 2026 the CGTP and several housing-rights movements gathered thousands of people in Lisbon, Porto, Coimbra and Faro. Tents were put up in Rossio, in Lisbon, and in Praça D. João I, in Porto. The Porto camp had already been cleared by police in the early hours of 15 October, an operation the organisers called disproportionate. The demands included caps on rents, restrictions on property funds buying hundreds of homes, and the repeal of measures such as the Golden Visa.

What it shows: the strength of joining unions and civic movements; the risk that violence, infiltration or provocation shifts the message away; and why a camp needs care, discipline and a negotiated exit.

Source: RTP, 18 Oct 2026 · Euronews, 17 Oct 2026

02 · MAY 2026

Teachers and the public school

On 16 May 2026 thousands of teachers marched from Cais do Sodré to Restauradores, in Lisbon, in a demonstration called by FENPROF against the revision of the teaching career statute and against the labour package. The chants — “A luta continua, nas escolas e na rua” and “Negociação sim, imposição não” — summed up the ask: a serious negotiating process, not an imposed decision.

What it shows: the importance of a clear procedural demand — negotiate — and not only a pay grievance. A demonstration can defend the dignity of a profession and the quality of a public service at the same time.

Source: RTP, 16 May 2026 · FENPROF, 15 May 2026

03 · FEBRUARY 2024

Farmers and roads

In February 2024 Portuguese farmers blocked roads and border crossings with tractors and slow marches, protesting against production costs, bureaucracy and the lack of support. Access to Lisbon was restricted. The agriculture minister met the protesters and a further meeting was scheduled; many groups stood down, on condition that they could return.

What it shows: economic and logistical pressure can open a negotiating table quickly. It also shows the risk of an action that hits people with no part in the decision — and the need to explain why that cost is fair.

Source: RTP, 2 Feb 2024 · Euronews, 1 Feb 2024

04 · SEPTEMBER 2024

Health and the NHS

In September 2024 doctors and nurses held two days of strike and demonstrated in front of the Ministry of Health, in Lisbon. They demanded better pay, better working conditions and more investment in the National Health Service. The two unions called the strike separately, but the coincidence of dates showed the shared discontent.

What it shows: a strike is not just “stopping”. It makes visible how much the system depends on the people who work in it. Legitimacy grows when the demand includes patients, professionals and the public service, not only pay.

Source: RTP, 22 Sep 2024

05 · FEBRUARY 2024

Racism and xenophobia

On 24 February 2024 demonstrations against racism and xenophobia took place in eight Portuguese cities: Braga, Coimbra, Faro, Guimarães, Lisbon, Portalegre, Porto and Viseu. The national coordination was the important part: the same cause spoke in several squares at once.

What it shows: a minority can build a majority when it finds allies and when the narrative links the concrete case to a general rule. It also shows the duty to protect those most exposed: visibility can bring pride and, at the same time, risk.

Source: Público, 24 Feb 2024

06 · NOVEMBER 2025

Climate and fossil fuels

On 22 November 2025 ten organisations, among them Climáximo and the Student Climate Strike, marched in Lisbon to demand an end to fossil fuels by 2030. The protest linked climate justice, youth and intergenerational responsibility.

What it shows: people who do not yet vote also have interests that need representation. The climate cause is a test for democracy: today's decisions distribute risk onto people who have not been born yet.

Source: RTP, 22 Nov 2025

07 · AUGUST 2023 → OCTOBER 2026

Lithium and Covas do Barroso

In August 2023 the village of Covas do Barroso rose against the exploitation of a lithium mine and set up a protest camp. In October 2026 residents blocked the mine works again, saying the project was being imposed on them. The conflict sets the energy transition, the territory, the water and a small community's right to decide its own future against one another.

What it shows: a local question can become national when it finds a larger question. It also shows the limit of consultation: being heard is not the same as having decision-making power.

Source: RTP, 11 Aug 2023 · Público, 9 Oct 2026

What these cases have in common

Strengths
  • A demand that can be explained in a few words.
  • An affected base that speaks for itself, not only intermediaries.
  • Alliances between movements, unions, specialists and communities.
  • A narrative that links the concrete case to a general rule.
  • The ability to negotiate without giving up dignity.
Risks
  • Violence or provocation changing the subject.
  • Infiltration by groups that do not respect the cause.
  • Repression treating the symptom and leaving the cause intact.
  • Exhaustion of the people most exposed.
  • Short memory once the media attention passes.
